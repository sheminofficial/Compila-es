# Lamentations

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold - A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The public-domain Protestant World English Bible is the source. This prose adaptation preserves the poems' grief, voices, images, theological tension, and unresolved ending. Read it alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 @@ The Lonely City

Jerusalem, once crowded and honored among nations, sat alone like a widow. She wept through the night, with no comforter among former allies; friends had betrayed her.

Judah went into exile after suffering and slavery. Roads to Zion mourned because festivals were empty. Priests groaned, young women grieved, and enemies prospered. The Lord had afflicted her for many rebellions; children marched captive.

Jerusalem remembered former treasures while her people fell. Enemies laughed at her ruin. Her uncleanness clung like a garment, and she had not considered her end. An enemy entered the sanctuary; people traded valuables for food.

The city cried to passersby: was any sorrow like hers? Fire entered her bones; a net caught her feet; her sins became a yoke. The Lord handed her defenders over and crushed Judah like grapes.

She stretched out her hands without comfort. She confessed the Lord was righteous because she rebelled, yet asked others to see her anguish. Priests and elders died seeking food.

Outside, the sword bereaved; inside, death waited. Enemies heard and rejoiced. Jerusalem asked God to bring the announced day and judge their wickedness too, while her heart remained faint under many sins.

## Chapter 2 @@ The Day of the Lord's Anger

The Lord covered daughter Zion with anger, casting Israel's splendor from heaven and not remembering his footstool. Strongholds, kingdom, princes, horn, and wall fell. He bent his bow like an enemy and poured fury like fire.

The sanctuary was treated like a garden shelter torn down. Festivals and Sabbaths were forgotten; king and priest rejected. The altar and sanctuary were abandoned, and enemies shouted in God's house as on a feast day.

Gates sank, bars broke, king and princes lived among nations, law disappeared, and prophets received no vision. Elders sat silent in dust; young women bowed to earth.

The poet's eyes failed with tears because children fainted in streets, asking mothers for grain and wine, dying in their arms.

What comparison could comfort Jerusalem? False prophets had offered empty visions instead of exposing guilt. Passersby mocked the city once called perfect beauty.

Zion should cry day and night, pouring out her heart and lifting hands for children starving at every street corner.

The poem confronted God with the horror: women ate children they had cared for; priest and prophet were killed in the sanctuary; young and old lay in streets. On the appointed day terror surrounded Jerusalem, and no survivor escaped.

## Chapter 3 @@ Hope in the Middle of Affliction

A suffering man spoke as one driven by God's wrath into darkness. His body wasted; chains enclosed him; prayers seemed blocked. God became like a bear and lion, tearing him apart, making him a target, filling him with bitterness and grinding his teeth in gravel.

Peace and hope seemed gone. Yet memory created a turn: because the Lord's loving kindness does not end, his mercies are new every morning. Great is his faithfulness. The Lord was the speaker's portion, so he would wait.

It is good to hope quietly for salvation, to bear the yoke while young, sit alone, and place one's mouth in dust. The Lord does not reject forever. Though he causes grief, he shows compassion according to abundant love; affliction is not his heart's delight.

God sees injustice: prisoners crushed, rights denied, justice twisted. Nothing occurs outside his command. Why should the living complain while sin remains? The people should examine their ways, return, lift hearts and hands, and confess rebellion.

The speaker again described unanswered prayer, tears, enemies, and a pit. From the lowest dungeon he called God's name. God came near and said, "Do not fear," defended his cause, and redeemed his life.

He asked God to see the enemies' insults, plots, and songs, and to repay them, pursuing them from under heaven.

*Original Scripture [Lamentations 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/LAM03.htm).*

## Chapter 4 @@ Gold Grown Dim

Gold had lost its shine; sacred stones lay scattered at street corners. Zion's precious children were treated like clay pots.

Even jackals fed their young, but the people became cruel under siege. Infants' tongues stuck from thirst; children begged for bread. Those once eating delicacies collapsed in streets, and those raised in scarlet embraced ash heaps.

Jerusalem's punishment surpassed Sodom's sudden ruin. Former nobles, once pure and radiant, became darker than soot, skin shriveled on bone. Death by sword seemed kinder than slow starvation.

Compassionate women cooked their children for food. The Lord poured out fierce anger, burning Zion's foundations. Kings of earth had not believed an enemy could enter Jerusalem's gates.

The fall came for prophets' sins and priests' violence against righteous blood. Defiled leaders wandered blindly; nations refused them refuge.

The people watched vainly for a nation unable to save. Their end arrived; pursuers were swifter than eagles and captured the Lord's anointed, under whose shadow they expected to live among nations.

Edom might rejoice temporarily, but judgment's cup would reach her. Zion's punishment would end and exile not continue forever; Edom's guilt would be exposed.

*Original Scripture [Lamentations 4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/LAM04.htm).*

## Chapter 5 @@ Remember Us

The community prayed: Lord, remember what happened and see their disgrace. Inheritance passed to strangers, homes to foreigners. They became fatherless, mothers like widows, paying for water and wood.

Pursuers pressed their necks; there was no rest. They sought bread from Egypt and Assyria. Ancestors sinned and died, while descendants carried consequences. Servants ruled over them with no rescuer.

Food was gathered at risk of the sword. Skin burned with famine. Women were violated in Zion and Judah's towns. Princes were hanged, elders dishonored, young men forced to grind grain, boys staggered under wood.

Elders vanished from gates and young people's music ceased. Joy left; dancing became mourning. The crown fell. The community confessed: woe to us, for we have sinned.

Their hearts fainted and eyes dimmed because Mount Zion lay desolate, prowled by foxes.

Yet the Lord reigns forever. Why, then, did he seem to forget and abandon them so long? They begged: restore us to yourself, Lord, and we will return; renew our days as long ago.

The book ended with a painful question rather than easy closure: had God utterly rejected them and remained exceedingly angry?

*Original Scripture [Lamentations 5](https://ebible.org/engwebp/LAM05.htm).*
