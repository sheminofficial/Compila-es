# 1 Peter

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold - A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The public-domain Protestant World English Bible is the source. Read this adaptation alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 @@ A Living Hope

Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ, wrote to chosen exiles scattered through Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia, chosen by the Father, made holy by the Spirit, and called to obey Jesus and share his sprinkled blood.

God had given them new birth into living hope through Jesus' resurrection and an unfading inheritance kept in heaven. God's power guarded them through faith, though varied trials briefly caused grief. Tested faith was more precious than refined gold and would result in praise when Christ appeared. Though unseen, they loved him and rejoiced while receiving faith's goal: salvation.

Prophets had searched this promised grace, and angels longed to understand it. Therefore they should prepare their minds, remain sober, and set hope fully on Christ's coming grace.

As obedient children they must be holy because God is holy. Their impartial Father judged each work, so they should live reverently. They were redeemed not with silver or gold but Christ's precious blood, like a spotless lamb.

Having been reborn through God's enduring word, they should love one another sincerely and deeply. Human glory fades like grass and flowers, but the Lord's word remains forever.

*Original Scripture [1 Peter 1](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1PE01.htm).*

## Chapter 2 @@ Living Stones

They must discard malice, deceit, hypocrisy, envy, and slander, craving pure spiritual milk so they might grow.

Coming to Christ, the living stone rejected by people but chosen by God, they too became living stones in a spiritual house and holy priesthood. Scripture called Christ a chosen cornerstone: trust in him would not lead to shame, but rejection made him a stumbling stone.

They were a chosen people, royal priesthood, holy nation, and God's possession, called from darkness into marvelous light. Once not a people and without mercy, they now belonged to God and had received mercy.

As exiles they should resist sinful desires and live honorably among the nations, so accusers might see good deeds and glorify God.

For the Lord's sake they should submit to human authorities, whether emperor or governors, doing good as free servants of God. They must honor everyone, love believers, fear God, and honor the king.

Household servants were told to submit even to harsh masters. Patient suffering for doing good followed Christ's example. He committed no sin or deceit, did not retaliate, entrusted himself to the just Judge, carried sins in his body on the tree, and healed them by his wounds. Once wandering sheep, they had returned to the Shepherd and Guardian of their souls.

*Original Scripture [1 Peter 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1PE02.htm).*

## Chapter 3 @@ A Defense Made with Gentleness

Wives were instructed to relate respectfully to husbands, so unbelieving husbands might be won without words by pure conduct. Their beauty should center not on decoration but the hidden person of a gentle, quiet spirit. Sarah, who obeyed Abraham, was an example of hopeful holiness.

Husbands must live considerately with wives, showing honor as fellow heirs of life's grace so prayers would not be hindered.

All should be united, compassionate, loving, tenderhearted, and humble. They must not repay evil or insult, but bless. Scripture promised that those loving life should restrain tongue and lips, turn from evil, seek peace, and remember the Lord watches the righteous.

Suffering for righteousness was blessed. They should not fear threats, but honor Christ as Lord and stay ready to explain their hope with gentleness, respect, and a clear conscience.

Christ suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, bringing them to God. Put to death in flesh and made alive in spirit, he proclaimed victory to imprisoned spirits. In Noah's days, eight people passed safely through water in the ark. Baptism corresponded to this, not as washing dirt but as an appeal of a good conscience through Jesus' resurrection. Christ was at God's right hand, with angels, authorities, and powers subjected to him.

*Original Scripture [1 Peter 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1PE03.htm).*

## Chapter 4 @@ Love Covers Many Sins

Since Christ suffered in flesh, believers should adopt his resolve and no longer live for human desires but God's will. Their former life of sensuality, drunkenness, feasts, and idolatry was enough. Former companions might insult them for refusing the same excess, but all would answer to the Judge of living and dead.

The end of all things was near. They should be sober and watchful in prayer. Above all, they must love deeply, because love covers many sins; offer hospitality without complaining; and use each received gift as faithful managers of God's varied grace. Speakers should speak God's words, servers rely on God's strength, so God receives glory through Christ.

They should not be surprised by fiery testing as though something strange happened. Sharing Christ's sufferings meant future joy when his glory appeared. Insult for Christ's name was blessed because God's Spirit rested upon them.

They must not suffer as murderers, thieves, evildoers, or meddlers. But suffering as Christians carried no shame; they should glorify God. Judgment began with God's household. Those suffering according to God's will should entrust themselves to the faithful Creator while continuing to do good.

*Original Scripture [1 Peter 4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1PE04.htm).*

## Chapter 5 @@ Stand Firm in Grace

Peter appealed to elders as a fellow elder, witness of Christ's sufferings, and sharer in coming glory. They should shepherd God's flock willingly, not for dishonest gain or domination, but as examples. When the Chief Shepherd appeared, they would receive an unfading crown.

Younger people should submit to elders, and everyone should wear humility toward one another. God opposes the proud and gives grace to the humble. They should humble themselves under God's strong hand, trusting him to lift them at the right time, and throw every anxiety upon him because he cared.

They must remain sober and watchful. Their adversary the devil prowled like a roaring lion seeking someone to devour. They should resist him, firm in faith, knowing believers worldwide shared such suffering.

After brief suffering, the God of all grace, who called them to eternal glory in Christ, would restore, establish, strengthen, and settle them. Dominion belonged to him forever.

Through Silvanus, whom Peter considered a faithful brother, he wrote briefly to testify that this was God's true grace: stand firm in it. The church in Babylon and Mark sent greetings. They should greet one another with a kiss of love. Peace to everyone in Christ.

*Original Scripture [1 Peter 5](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1PE05.htm).*
