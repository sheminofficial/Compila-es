# Ezra

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold - A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The public-domain Protestant World English Bible is the source. Lists are condensed in prose while their purpose, representative names, and stated totals are preserved. Read this adaptation alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 @@ Cyrus Opens the Way Home

In Cyrus of Persia's first year, the Lord fulfilled Jeremiah's word by stirring the king to issue a written proclamation throughout his empire.

Cyrus declared that the Lord, God of heaven, had given him earthly kingdoms and appointed him to build a house in Jerusalem. Any member of God's people could return, with neighbors supplying silver, gold, goods, animals, and voluntary gifts.

Heads of Judah and Benjamin's families, priests, Levites, and everyone whose spirit God stirred prepared to go. Neighbors strengthened them with valuables and offerings.

Cyrus returned temple objects Nebuchadnezzar had taken from Jerusalem and placed in his god's house. Mithredath the treasurer counted them to Sheshbazzar, Judah's prince: gold and silver dishes and other articles, 5,400 in all, carried from Babylon to Jerusalem.

*Original Scripture [Ezra 1](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EZR01.htm).*

## Chapter 2 @@ The Returning Community

The exiles returned from Babylon to Jerusalem and Judah under Zerubbabel, Jeshua, Nehemiah, Seraiah, Reelaiah, Mordecai, and other leaders, each family to its town.

The record named families and towns, then priests, Levites, singers descended from Asaph, gatekeepers, temple servants, and descendants of Solomon's servants.

Some families could not prove ancestry. Priestly claimants from Habaiah, Hakkoz, and Barzillai were excluded from sacred food until a priest could consult Urim and Thummim.

The whole assembly numbered 42,360, besides 7,337 servants and 200 singers. They brought 736 horses, 245 mules, 435 camels, and 6,720 donkeys.

At the Lord's house, family heads gave according to ability: 61,000 gold darics, 5,000 silver minas, and 100 priestly garments.

Priests, Levites, people, singers, gatekeepers, and temple servants settled in their towns.

*Original Scripture [Ezra 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EZR02.htm).*

## Chapter 3 @@ The Altar before the Foundation

In the seventh month, the people gathered as one in Jerusalem. Jeshua son of Jozadak, fellow priests, and Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel rebuilt Israel's altar despite fear of neighboring peoples.

They offered morning and evening burnt offerings according to Moses' law and celebrated the Festival of Shelters with daily sacrifices. From the month's first day offerings resumed, though the temple foundation was not laid.

They paid masons and carpenters and supplied food, drink, and oil to Sidon and Tyre for cedar from Lebanon by sea to Joppa, authorized by Cyrus.

In the second year, Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and the returned community appointed Levites aged twenty and older to supervise construction.

When builders laid the foundation, priests with trumpets and Asaph's Levites with cymbals praised the Lord: he is good; his faithful love endures.

People shouted, but older priests and family heads who remembered the former temple wept loudly. Joy and grief blended so completely that their sound carried far away.

*Original Scripture [Ezra 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EZR03.htm).*

## Chapter 4 @@ The Work Is Forced to Stop

Enemies of Judah and Benjamin offered to build, claiming they had sacrificed to Israel's God since Esar Haddon settled them there. Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and family heads refused: Cyrus had assigned the work to the returned community.

The surrounding peoples discouraged builders, frightened them, and hired advisers from Cyrus's reign through Darius's.

During Ahasuerus's reign they wrote an accusation; under Artaxerxes, Bishlam, Mithredath, Tabeel, and then Rehum and Shimshai sent an Aramaic letter.

They warned the king that rebellious Jerusalem was rebuilding walls and would stop paying tribute. A search found the city's history of powerful kings and revolts.

Artaxerxes ordered the work stopped until further command. Rehum and companions hurried to Jerusalem and used force.

Temple work ceased until Darius of Persia's second year.

*Original Scripture [Ezra 4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EZR04.htm).*

## Chapter 5 @@ The Prophets Call the Builders Back

Haggai and Zechariah son of Iddo prophesied to Judah and Jerusalem in Israel's God's name. Zerubbabel and Jeshua restarted the temple, supported by the prophets.

Tattenai, governor Beyond the River, Shethar Bozenai, and associates asked who authorized the building and requested the leaders' names.

God watched over the elders, so officials did not stop them while a report went to Darius.

Tattenai described the large-stone, timbered structure and diligent work. The elders explained that they served the God of heaven and earth. Their ancestors had provoked him, so Nebuchadnezzar destroyed the temple and exiled them.

Cyrus later ordered the temple rebuilt and returned its vessels through Sheshbazzar, who laid the foundation. Work had continued but remained unfinished.

Tattenai asked Darius to search Babylon's archives for Cyrus's decree and send his decision.

*Original Scripture [Ezra 5](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EZR05.htm).*

## Chapter 6 @@ The Temple Is Finished

Darius ordered a search. At Ecbatana in Media, a memorandum confirmed Cyrus's decree: rebuild the Jerusalem temple at royal expense and restore its vessels.

Darius commanded Tattenai and associates to stay away, pay construction costs promptly from provincial taxes, and supply bulls, rams, lambs, grain, salt, wine, and oil for daily offerings and prayers for king and sons.

Anyone altering the decree would be impaled on a beam from his house. The God who placed his name there would overthrow interference.

The officials obeyed. Encouraged by Haggai and Zechariah, elders completed the temple under Israel's God and Persian kings Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes. It finished on Adar's third day in Darius's sixth year.

They dedicated it joyfully with 100 bulls, 200 rams, 400 lambs, and twelve male goats, assigning priests and Levites.

They kept Passover and Unleavened Bread for seven days. The Lord gave joy and turned the Assyrian king's heart toward them.

*Original Scripture [Ezra 6](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EZR06.htm).*

## Chapter 7 @@ Ezra Arrives with the King's Letter

In Artaxerxes's seventh year, Ezra traveled from Babylon. He descended from Aaron through the priestly line and was a skilled scribe in Moses' law. The king granted his requests because God's hand was upon him.

Priests, Levites, singers, gatekeepers, temple servants, and Israelites reached Jerusalem with Ezra in the fifth month after a four-month journey.

Ezra had devoted his heart to study the Lord's law, practice it, and teach its statutes.

Artaxerxes's Aramaic letter authorized willing Israelites to go, carry royal and voluntary silver and gold, buy offerings, and use remaining funds wisely. Treasurers Beyond the River must provide silver, wheat, wine, oil, and salt; temple workers were tax-exempt.

Ezra was to appoint judges and teach God's law. Disobedience could bring death, banishment, confiscation, or imprisonment.

Ezra blessed God for putting temple honor in the king's heart and giving him faithful love. Strengthened by God's hand, he gathered leaders.

*Original Scripture [Ezra 7](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EZR07.htm).*

## Chapter 8 @@ Safe Journey without Soldiers

Ezra listed family leaders returning with him, including descendants of Phinehas, Ithamar, David, Parosh, Pahath Moab, Zattu, Adin, Elam, Shephatiah, Joab, Bani, Bebai, Azgad, Adonikam, Bigvai, and others.

At the Ahava canal he found no Levites. He sent leaders to Iddo at Casiphia, and God's good hand brought Sherebiah, Hashabiah, Jeshaiah, relatives, and 220 temple servants.

Ezra proclaimed a fast to seek a straight journey for families and possessions. He was ashamed to request soldiers after telling the king God's hand favored seekers and opposed deserters. God heard.

He entrusted weighed silver, gold, and vessels to twelve priestly leaders, charging them to guard these holy gifts. In Jerusalem, after three days, everything was reweighed and recorded.

The travelers offered twelve bulls, ninety-six rams, seventy-seven lambs, and twelve goats. They delivered royal orders, and officials supported people and temple.

*Original Scripture [Ezra 8](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EZR08.htm).*

## Chapter 9 @@ Ezra's Prayer of Confession

Officials reported that Israelites, priests, and Levites had not separated from surrounding peoples' practices and had intermarried; leaders were foremost in unfaithfulness.

Ezra tore his clothes and robe, pulled hair from head and beard, and sat appalled. Those trembling at God's word gathered until evening sacrifice.

Ezra rose, knelt, spread his hands, and confessed shame. Guilt had risen above their heads, from ancestors' days through exile, sword, captivity, plunder, and disgrace.

For a brief moment God had shown grace: preserving a remnant, giving a secure place in the sanctuary, brightening eyes, and granting relief in slavery. Persian kings had allowed temple restoration and protection.

Yet they abandoned commands warning that the land was polluted and forbidding alliances designed to seek surrounding prosperity.

God punished less than their guilt deserved and preserved survivors. Renewed disobedience could bring complete destruction.

Ezra ended before Israel's righteous God: they remained only as a guilty remnant unable to stand.

*Original Scripture [Ezra 9](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EZR09.htm).*

## Chapter 10 @@ A Difficult Community Decision

While Ezra prayed and wept before the temple, a great crowd gathered, weeping bitterly.

Shecaniah son of Jehiel admitted unfaithfulness through foreign marriages but said hope remained. He proposed a covenant to send away the women and children according to law and counsel, urging Ezra to act courageously.

Ezra made leaders swear, then withdrew fasting and mourning. A proclamation ordered all returned exiles to Jerusalem within three days or lose property and community membership.

In heavy ninth-month rain, the assembly trembled. Ezra said they had increased guilt and must separate. The people agreed but requested an orderly investigation because the crowd was large, rain severe, and offense widespread.

Jonathan son of Asahel and Jahzeiah son of Tikvah opposed the procedure, supported by Meshullam and Shabbethai. Nevertheless appointed family heads examined cases from the tenth month's first day to the first month's first day.

The book concluded with a list of priests, Levites, singers, gatekeepers, and Israelites involved, beginning with Jeshua's descendants and ending by noting that some marriages had children.

*Original Scripture [Ezra 10](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EZR10.htm).*
