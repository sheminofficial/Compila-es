# 3 John

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold — A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The Protestant World English Bible is the public-domain source. This letter is retold in accessible English and is intended to be read alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 — Open Doors and Closed Hearts

The elder wrote to his beloved friend Gaius, a man he loved in truth. His first words were a prayer: may everything go well with you, may your body be healthy, and may your outward life prosper just as your soul does.

Traveling believers had brought the elder good news. They spoke about Gaius’s faithfulness to the truth, and their report matched the way he lived. Nothing gave the elder greater joy than hearing that his spiritual children were walking in truth.

Gaius’s faith could be seen in his hospitality. He served fellow believers even when they arrived as strangers. These travelers later told the church about his love. The elder encouraged him to send them onward in a manner worthy of God, supplying what they needed for the next part of their journey.

They had gone out for the sake of the Name—the name of Jesus—and had accepted no support from people who did not believe. Christians therefore ought to welcome and help people like them. By supporting their journey, believers could become fellow workers for the truth, even if they did not travel themselves.

But another man had chosen a different path.

The elder had written to the church before, but Diotrephes would not accept his authority. Diotrephes loved to be first. If the elder came in person, he intended to call attention to what this man was doing. Diotrephes spread wicked accusations against him. Nor did the damage end with words. He refused to welcome the traveling brothers, stopped others who wanted to receive them, and forced those generous believers out of the church.

The contrast could hardly have been sharper. Gaius opened his door to serve the truth. Diotrephes closed the door because he wanted control.

“Do not imitate what is evil,” the elder urged his friend. “Imitate what is good.” A person who does good belongs to God; a person who continues in evil shows that he has not truly seen God.

Then the elder mentioned Demetrius. Everyone spoke well of him, and his life agreed with the truth itself. The elder added his own testimony, and Gaius knew that his testimony could be trusted.

There was much more to discuss. Once again, the elder did not want to handle it all with ink and pen. He hoped to see Gaius soon, when they could talk face to face.

He ended with peace. The friends with him sent their greetings, and Gaius was to greet each friend there by name. Against the pride of a man who wanted to stand above everyone, the final words offered a quieter picture of Christian community: truth lived openly, travelers welcomed, goodness imitated, and friends remembered one by one.
