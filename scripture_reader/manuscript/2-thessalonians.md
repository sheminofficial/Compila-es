# 2 Thessalonians

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold — A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The Protestant World English Bible is the public-domain source. The letter’s judgment language and difficult prophecy about rebellion, restraint, and the lawless one are preserved without assigning them to a modern person, institution, or timetable. This adaptation is intended to be read alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 — Faith That Kept Growing

Paul, Silvanus, and Timothy wrote again to the church in Thessalonica, a community belonging to God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

“Grace and peace to you,” they began.

They felt obligated to thank God for these believers—and the gratitude was not a polite opening. Their faith was growing beyond expectation, and their love for one another kept increasing. Paul and his companions spoke proudly about them in other churches.

What made that growth remarkable was its setting. The Thessalonians were enduring persecutions and many forms of suffering. Still, they persevered and trusted God.

Their endurance pointed toward God’s righteous judgment. Their suffering did not mean that God’s kingdom had rejected them. They were suffering for that kingdom, and God would count them worthy of the calling they had received.

Justice would not remain upside down forever. God would answer those who afflicted his people, and he would give relief to those who were afflicted, including Paul and his companions.

That relief was connected with the revelation of the Lord Jesus from heaven. He would appear with mighty angels and flaming fire. The letter described judgment for those who refused to know God and would not obey the good news of Jesus. They would face eternal destruction away from the Lord’s presence and the glory of his power.

On that same day, Jesus would be glorified among his holy people and regarded with wonder by all who had believed. The Thessalonians belonged among them because they had believed the testimony brought to them.

Paul did not use that hope to encourage revenge. He turned it into prayer.

He and his friends continually asked God to make the Thessalonians worthy of their calling. May God, by his power, bring every good desire and every work arising from faith to completion.

Then the name of the Lord Jesus would be honored through their lives, and they would share in his honor—not as a reward they had earned alone, but according to the grace of God and the Lord Jesus Christ.

Their persecutors were not the center of the prayer. Christ was. The believers’ task was not to control the day of judgment, but to let God’s power complete goodness, faith, and love in them while they waited.

## Chapter 2 — Do Not Be Quickly Shaken

A troubling report had reached the church. Some message—perhaps a spiritual claim, spoken word, or letter falsely presented as apostolic—said that the day of Christ had already arrived.

Paul asked the believers not to let such claims shake their minds or throw them into fear. Their gathering to the Lord Jesus at his coming remained their hope, but they must not be deceived about it.

A rebellion would come first. Then a figure Paul called the man of sin and son of destruction would be revealed. This lawless person would oppose every object of worship and exalt himself above them. He would take his seat in God’s temple and present himself as God.

Paul reminded the Thessalonians that he had spoken about these matters while he was still with them. They knew something his surviving letter does not fully explain: what restrained the lawless one until the proper time.

The mystery of lawlessness was already active, but a restrainer still held it back. The restraint would remain until the restrainer was removed from the way. Then the lawless one would be revealed.

Paul did not leave that figure as an equal rival to Christ. The Lord Jesus would overthrow him with the breath of his mouth and bring him to nothing through the brightness of his coming.

Before that destruction, the lawless one’s arrival would display Satan’s work—power, signs, and wonders designed to lie. Wicked deception would capture people who were perishing because they had refused to love the truth that could save them.

The letter spoke severely: because they rejected truth and delighted in wrongdoing, God would give them over to a powerful delusion. Those who chose the lie over truth would face judgment.

Then Paul turned from the deceived to the church he loved.

He thanked God for them. The Lord loved them, and God had chosen them for salvation through the Spirit’s sanctifying work and their belief in the truth. The good news had called them toward sharing in the glory of Jesus Christ.

Therefore they were to stand firm. They should hold to the teaching they had genuinely received from the apostles, whether spoken in person or written in a letter.

Paul ended the section with a blessing. May Jesus Christ himself and God the Father—who loved them and freely gave eternal comfort and good hope—comfort their hearts and establish them in every good word and work.

The chapter does not identify the restrainer for later readers or give permission to attach the lawless one’s title to any disliked public figure. Its practical command is clearer than its unanswered details: do not panic, do not accept a false message, love truth, stand firm, and continue doing good.

*Original Scripture [2 Thessalonians 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/2TH02.htm).*

## Chapter 3 — Do Not Grow Weary of Doing Good

Paul asked the Thessalonians to pray for him and his companions. May the Lord’s message run swiftly and be honored elsewhere as it had been among them. May the messengers be rescued from unreasonable and evil people, because not everyone had faith.

Human beings could be faithless. The Lord remained faithful. He would strengthen the church and guard them from the evil one.

Paul’s confidence in the believers rested in the Lord. He trusted that they were obeying his instructions and would continue to do so. May the Lord guide their hearts into God’s love and the perseverance shown by Christ.

Then Paul addressed disorder inside the church.

Believers were to step back from a member who lived rebelliously and rejected the pattern of teaching received from the apostles. Paul reminded them of the example his team had given in Thessalonica. They had not lived irresponsibly. They did not demand free meals from others. They labored and struggled day and night so that they would not become a financial burden.

As apostles they could have claimed support, but they chose work in order to offer the church a pattern worth imitating.

Paul repeated a rule he had taught while present: if someone was not willing to work, that person should not expect others to provide his food.

The issue was unwillingness, not inability. Reports said that some people in the church refused ordinary work while staying very busy in everyone else’s affairs. Paul commanded and urged them in the Lord Jesus to settle down, work quietly, and eat bread earned through their own labor.

To the rest of the church he said, “Do not grow tired of doing what is right.” Discipline toward the unwilling must not become an excuse to abandon generosity toward people in genuine need.

If someone refused the instruction in this letter, the community should take note and withdraw close company so that the person might feel shame and change. Even then, they were not to treat him as an enemy. The correction belonged inside a family: warn him as a brother.

Paul prayed that the Lord of peace would give them peace at every time and in every way. May the Lord be with them all.

Then he took the writing instrument himself. The final greeting came in Paul’s own handwriting, a sign he used in every letter—particularly meaningful after the earlier warning about a letter falsely claiming to come from him.

“The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all.”

The church waited for Christ not through panic, speculation, or interference in other people’s lives, but through prayer, steady work, faithful correction, peace, and goodness that refused to become exhausted.

*Original Scripture [2 Thessalonians 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/2TH03.htm).*
