# Micah

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold - A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The public-domain Protestant World English Bible is the source. Read this adaptation alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 @@ The Lord Comes Down

The Lord's word came to Micah of Moresheth during the reigns of Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, concerning Samaria and Jerusalem.

All peoples should listen: the Lord rose from his holy temple as witness. He came down, and mountains melted like wax while valleys split like water. This happened because of Jacob's rebellion and Israel's sin, centered in Samaria and Jerusalem.

Samaria would become rubble in a field, its stones poured into the valley. Its idols and wages of prostitution would burn.

Micah mourned barefoot and stripped, howling like jackals, because Samaria's wound was incurable and had reached Jerusalem's gate.

A funeral wordplay swept through towns: Gath must not hear; Beth Ophrah rolled in dust; Shaphir passed naked; Zaanan did not come out; Beth Ezel lost support; Maroth waited for good while disaster came from the Lord.

Lachish should harness swift horses; she had led Zion into sin. Moresheth Gath received farewell gifts, and Achzib proved deceptive. A conqueror would reach Mareshah, and Israel's glory retreat to Adullam.

Judah should cut its hair in mourning for beloved children carried into exile.

*Original Scripture [Micah 1](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MIC01.htm).*

## Chapter 2 @@ Woe to Those Who Plan Injustice

Woe to those planning evil in bed and carrying it out at morning because they possessed power. They coveted fields and houses, seizing inheritance and oppressing families.

The Lord planned disaster they could not escape. Their land would be divided, and a mocking lament would announce complete ruin.

They told true prophets not to speak of disgrace. But the Lord's words did good to upright people. His people had risen like enemies, stripping peaceful travelers, driving women from pleasant homes, and taking blessing from children.

They must leave because the land was polluted and would destroy them. A prophet promising wine and strong drink would be welcomed.

Yet God promised to gather Jacob's remnant like sheep in a fold, noisy with people. The One who breaks open the way would go before them; their king would pass through the gate, with the Lord at their head.

*Original Scripture [Micah 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MIC02.htm).*

## Chapter 3 @@ Leaders Who Hate Justice

Jacob's rulers should have known justice, yet they hated good and loved evil, pictured as tearing skin and flesh from God's people and cooking them in a pot. When they cried, God would hide his face because of their deeds.

False prophets announced peace when fed but declared war against anyone who gave them nothing. Night would cover their visions, and they would be ashamed when God gave no answer.

Micah, by contrast, was filled with power, justice, and courage through the Lord's Spirit to declare Jacob's rebellion.

Leaders judged for bribes, priests taught for payment, and prophets told fortunes for money, yet claimed the Lord protected them.

Because of them, Zion would be plowed like a field, Jerusalem become ruins, and the temple mountain become a wooded height.

*Original Scripture [Micah 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MIC03.htm).*

## Chapter 4 @@ Swords into Plowshares

In later days the Lord's temple mountain would rise above hills. Nations would stream upward, asking to learn God's ways. Instruction would go from Zion and the Lord's word from Jerusalem.

God would judge among peoples. They would hammer swords into plowshares and spears into pruning hooks. Nations would stop training for war. Everyone would sit safely under vine and fig tree.

Though peoples followed their gods, Israel would walk in the Lord's name. God would gather the lame, exiled, and afflicted, making a strong nation, and reign from Mount Zion.

Jerusalem's former dominion would return. Yet first she would writhe like a woman in labor, leave the city, live in the field, and go to Babylon. There the Lord would rescue her.

Many nations gathered against Zion, unaware of God's plan. Daughter Zion would rise and thresh them with iron horn and bronze hoofs, dedicating their wealth to the Lord of all earth.

*Original Scripture [Micah 4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MIC04.htm).*

## Chapter 5 @@ The Ruler from Bethlehem

Jerusalem should gather for siege; Israel's judge would be struck on the cheek.

But Bethlehem Ephrathah, small among Judah's clans, would produce a ruler for Israel whose origins reached ancient days. God would give his people up until the woman in labor gave birth and the ruler's brothers returned.

He would stand and shepherd in the Lord's strength and majesty. His people would live securely, and his greatness reach earth's ends. He would be their peace.

If Assyria invaded, God would raise sufficient shepherds and leaders to deliver. Jacob's remnant among nations would be like dew from the Lord, not waiting for humans, and like a lion among flocks, powerful against enemies.

God would remove warhorses, chariots, cities, strongholds, sorceries, fortune tellers, carved images, sacred pillars, and Asherah poles, purifying dependence and idolatry. He would execute vengeance on nations that refused obedience.

*Original Scripture [Micah 5](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MIC05.htm).*

## Chapter 6 @@ What the Lord Requires

The Lord brought a covenant case before mountains and ancient foundations. What wrong had he done to weary his people?

He had brought them from Egypt, redeemed them from slavery, and sent Moses, Aaron, and Miriam. They should remember Balak king of Moab and Balaam son of Beor, and the journey from Shittim to Gilgal, recognizing God's saving acts.

What offering could approach God - burnt offerings, thousands of rams, rivers of oil, even a firstborn child? God had already shown what is good: do justice, love mercy, and walk humbly with him.

The Lord called the city to hear. Wicked scales, dishonest weights, violent rich people, lies, and deceit remained. Therefore eating would not satisfy, saving would fail, sowing would not yield, and pressed olives and grapes would not be enjoyed.

They followed Omri's practices and Ahab's house, so they would become desolate and bear peoples' scorn.

*Original Scripture [Micah 6](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MIC06.htm).*

## Chapter 7 @@ Who Is a God Like You?

Micah grieved like someone seeking fruit after harvest and finding none. The faithful had vanished; people hunted one another. Officials, judges, and powerful people coordinated evil. The best resembled thorns.

Trust had collapsed even among neighbors, friends, and households. Yet Micah would watch for the Lord and wait for the God of salvation.

An enemy should not celebrate his fall. Though he sat in darkness, the Lord would be his light. He would bear God's indignation because he had sinned until God defended his cause and brought him into light.

Jerusalem's walls would be rebuilt and boundaries extended. People would return from Assyria, Egypt, sea, and mountain, though the earth first became desolate because of its inhabitants' deeds.

The prophet prayed that God would shepherd his inheritance as in ancient days, showing wonders like the exodus. Nations would see, lose strength, and approach trembling.

Who is a God like the Lord, forgiving guilt and passing over the remnant's rebellion? He does not remain angry forever but delights in faithful love. He would again show compassion, trample sins, and throw wrongdoing into the sea's depths, keeping truth to Jacob and covenant love to Abraham.

*Original Scripture [Micah 7](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MIC07.htm).*
