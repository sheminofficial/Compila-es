# 2 Peter

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold — A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The Protestant World English Bible is the public-domain source. The letter’s difficult examples of judgment, warnings about false teachers, and cosmic images of the day of the Lord are preserved without turning them into a speculative timetable. This adaptation is intended to be read alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 — A Lamp in a Dark Place

Simon Peter introduced himself as a servant and apostle of Jesus Christ. He wrote to people who had received the same precious faith as the apostles through the righteousness of their God and Savior, Jesus Christ.

May grace and peace multiply, he prayed, as they came to know God and Jesus their Lord.

God’s divine power had already provided everything needed for life and godliness. These gifts came through knowing the one who called them by his own glory and goodness. God had also given precious and very great promises. Through them, believers could share in the life God gives and escape the world’s corruption, which grew from disordered desire.

Because of that gift, they were not to remain passive.

They should apply every effort to their faith, adding moral excellence. Excellence should grow with knowledge; knowledge with self-control; self-control with perseverance; perseverance with godliness; godliness with affection for fellow believers; and that affection with love.

When these qualities were present and increasing, knowledge of Jesus would not remain idle or fruitless. But a person who lacked them had become short-sighted, almost blind, forgetting that former sins had been cleansed.

Peter urged his brothers and sisters to take their calling and election seriously. By practicing these things, they would not stumble, and a rich entrance would be supplied into the eternal kingdom of Jesus Christ.

The readers already knew this truth and were established in it. Peter intended to remind them anyway. As long as he remained in the “tent” of his body, he believed it right to wake them through remembrance. Jesus had made clear to him that he would soon lay that tent aside. Peter therefore worked so that, even after his departure, they would be able to remember.

The apostles had not invented clever myths when they announced the power and coming of Jesus. Peter said they had witnessed his majesty.

He recalled the holy mountain. Jesus received honor and glory from God the Father, and a voice came from the Majestic Glory: this was God’s beloved Son, the one who pleased him. Peter had heard that heavenly voice while standing there with Jesus.

The prophetic word also deserved close attention. It was like a lamp shining in a dark place until daylight came and the morning star rose in their hearts.

Scripture’s prophecy was not produced by a prophet’s private origin or will. Human beings did not create it simply because they decided to speak. Holy people spoke from God as the Holy Spirit carried them along.

Peter faced death by helping the church remember: remember the grace already given, cultivate a life that bears fruit, remember the witnessed glory of Jesus, and keep Scripture’s lamp burning while the world is dark.

## Chapter 2 — Wells without Water

False prophets had appeared among God’s people before, and false teachers would also enter the churches. They would bring destructive teaching secretly, even denying the Master who had bought them. In doing so, they would bring swift destruction upon themselves.

Many would follow their immoral behavior, causing outsiders to speak against the way of truth. Greed would drive these teachers to exploit believers with carefully invented words. Their judgment was not asleep, and their destruction was not late.

Peter gathered examples of both judgment and rescue.

God did not spare angels when they sinned, but confined them in Tartarus, in dark pits, to await judgment. He did not spare the ancient violent world, yet he preserved Noah—a preacher of righteousness—with seven others when the flood came.

God reduced Sodom and Gomorrah to ashes as a warning about ungodliness. Yet he rescued Lot, who was deeply distressed by the shameless life around him. Day after day, what Lot saw and heard tormented his righteous soul.

The conclusion was that the Lord knew how to rescue godly people from trial and how to keep the unrighteous for judgment.

The false teachers followed corrupt desire and despised authority. They were bold and self-willed, insulting glorious beings without fear. Angels, though greater in strength, did not bring such abusive accusations before the Lord.

These teachers spoke against things they did not understand. Peter compared them to animals acting without reason and moving toward capture and destruction. The harm they practiced would return upon them.

They treated open daytime indulgence as pleasure. They joined the believers’ meals while becoming stains in the community. Their eyes searched for adultery; they could not stop sinning. They baited unstable people and trained their hearts in greed.

They had left the right road and followed Balaam son of Beor, who loved profit gained through wrongdoing. Balaam’s disobedience was stopped when a donkey—normally unable to speak—used a human voice to restrain the prophet’s madness.

The teachers were wells without water and clouds driven by a storm: impressive promises with nothing life-giving inside. They spoke enormous empty words and used uncontrolled desire to lure people who had only recently escaped a life of error.

They promised freedom while living as slaves of corruption. Whatever overcomes a person becomes that person’s master.

Knowledge of Jesus had allowed them to escape the world’s defilement. If they returned, became entangled, and were overcome again, their last condition would be worse than the first. It would have been better not to know the righteous way than to know it and turn away from the holy command entrusted to them.

Two harsh proverbs described the return: a dog going back to its vomit, and a washed pig returning to roll in mud.

The chapter’s severity matched the danger. These were not teachers who merely made an innocent mistake. Their greed, abuse, deceit, sexual corruption, and rejection of Christ trapped others while they advertised freedom.

## Chapter 3 — Patience Is Not Slowness

This was Peter’s second letter to his beloved readers. In both letters he aimed to awaken sincere thinking through reminders. They should remember the earlier words of the holy prophets and the command delivered by the apostles of the Lord and Savior.

Mockers would come in the last days. They would follow their own desires and ask what had happened to the promise of Christ’s coming. Generations had died, they argued, while everything seemed to continue exactly as it had from creation’s beginning.

Their claim depended on deliberate forgetting.

Long ago, heavens existed and earth was formed from water and through water by God’s word. Through water, the ancient world was flooded and perished. The present heavens and earth were being kept by that same divine word, now reserved for fire and the day when ungodliness would be judged.

Believers must not forget another truth: with the Lord, one day can be like a thousand years and a thousand years like one day. God’s relation to time could not be measured by human impatience.

The Lord was not slow about his promise. What looked like delay was patience. He did not desire people to perish, but to come to repentance.

Still, the day of the Lord would arrive unexpectedly, like a thief. The heavens would pass with a roar. The elements would dissolve in fierce heat, and earth with its works would be exposed and burned.

Peter did not offer this image merely to satisfy curiosity about the future. He asked what kind of people the readers should become if the present order would not last.

Their lives should be holy and devoted to God as they looked for the day of God. The heavens would burn and the elements melt, but God’s promise directed them beyond destruction: new heavens and a new earth where righteousness made its home.

Because they waited for that world, believers should work to be found at peace, spotless and blameless before God. They should understand the Lord’s patience as an opportunity for salvation.

Peter noted that their beloved brother Paul had written about these matters with wisdom given by God. Some parts of Paul’s letters were difficult to understand. Ignorant and unstable people twisted those passages, just as they twisted other Scriptures, bringing destruction upon themselves.

Forewarned believers must remain alert. They should not be swept away by lawless error and fall from their steady position.

Instead, they were to grow—in the grace and knowledge of Jesus Christ, their Lord and Savior.

Glory belonged to him now and into the day of eternity.
