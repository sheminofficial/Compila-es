## Chapter 1 — Truth with Love

The writer introduced himself simply as “the elder.” He addressed his short letter to the chosen lady and her children. Those words may refer to a Christian woman and her family, or they may picture a local church and its members; the letter itself does not settle the question. What it makes clear is the elder’s love for them—a love shared by everyone who knew the truth.

Truth was not a passing idea to him. It lived within God’s people and would remain with them forever. For that reason, he greeted his readers with confidence: grace, mercy, and peace would be with them from God the Father and from Jesus Christ, the Father’s Son, in truth and love.

The elder had recently met some of the lady’s children, or members of the church, and discovered that they were walking in truth. Their daily lives followed the command they had received from the Father. This brought him great joy.

His main request was both old and always necessary: love one another. He was not offering a new invention. This command had belonged to their faith from the beginning.

Love, however, was more than warm language. To love God was to walk according to his commands. The command to love and the call to obedience could not be separated. They were meant to shape the road a believer walked each day.

A warning followed. Many deceivers had gone out into the world. They refused to confess that Jesus Christ truly came in the flesh. The elder described such teaching as the work of the deceiver and the antichrist. His readers needed to watch themselves carefully, so that the work already done among them would not be lost and they could receive a full reward.

Anyone who rushed beyond Christ’s teaching instead of remaining in it did not have God. The person who remained in that teaching had both the Father and the Son.

The warning affected hospitality too. Traveling teachers often depended on homes for shelter and support. If someone arrived but did not bring the teaching of Christ, the believers were not to receive that person into the house or offer a greeting that supported the false mission. Helping such work would make them partners in the harm it caused. Love did not require them to assist deception.

The elder had much more he wanted to say, but he would not try to fit everything onto paper with ink. He hoped to visit and speak face to face. A living conversation would make their joy complete.

The letter closed with greetings from the children of the reader’s chosen sister. It was brief enough to carry in one hand, yet it held together two responsibilities that must never be pulled apart: remain firm in truth, and walk in love.
