# Jonah

Manuscript for *The Bible, Retold — A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. This retelling is based on the public-domain Protestant World English Bible. Dialogue and poetry are recast from the passages cited. Read alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 — A Ship for Tarshish

The Lord spoke to Jonah son of Amittai. He was to get up and go to Nineveh, a great city whose evil had come before God. There he was to speak out against what the people were doing.

Jonah got up. At Joppa he found a ship bound for Tarshish, paid for his passage, and went aboard. He meant to get away from the Lord.

It was the Lord who sent the wind over the sea. The storm grew so fierce that the ship was in danger of breaking apart. Each sailor cried out to his own god. They threw the cargo overboard, trying to make the ship lighter. By then Jonah had gone as far below deck as he could and lay fast asleep.

The captain came down and found him. “How can you sleep? Get up and call to your god. Perhaps he’ll take notice of us and keep us alive.”

On deck the sailors agreed to cast lots to find out who had brought this trouble on them. The lot fell to Jonah. Questions came at him one after another. Who had caused this? What work did he do? Where had he come from? What country and what people did he belong to?

“I’m a Hebrew,” he said. “I worship the Lord, the God of heaven. He made the sea and the dry land.”

They already knew from Jonah himself that he was running away from the Lord. Now they were terrified. “What have you done?”

The sea grew rougher. They asked Jonah what they could do with him to make it quiet.

“Pick me up and throw me into the sea. It’ll go still for you. I know I’m the reason this storm has come upon you.”

The men bent to their oars. They tried to row back to land, but the sea kept rising against them. At last they called out to the Lord, begging him to spare their lives and not hold them guilty for shedding innocent blood. What had happened, they said, was what the Lord had chosen to do.

They lifted Jonah and threw him overboard. The sea stopped raging. The men were filled with fear of the Lord. They offered him a sacrifice and made vows to him.

The Lord provided a huge fish to swallow Jonah. For three days and three nights, Jonah was inside it.

## Chapter 2 — The Prayer from Below

From inside the fish, Jonah prayed to the Lord his God.

> I called to the Lord in my distress,  
> and he answered me.  
> From the belly of Sheol I called for help.  
> You heard me.
>
> You threw me down into the depths,  
> into the very heart of the sea.  
> The current closed around me.  
> Your waves rolled over my head, one after another.
>
> I thought you had driven me out of your sight.  
> Yet I would look again toward your holy temple.
>
> The water closed over me and took my breath.  
> The deep surrounded me.  
> Weeds wound around my head.  
> I sank to the roots of the mountains.  
> The earth shut its bars behind me, as if forever.  
> But you drew me alive out of the pit,  
> Lord, my God.
>
> As my strength failed, I remembered you.  
> My prayer reached you there, in your holy temple.  
> Those who cling to empty idols  
> give up the mercy they could have received.
>
> I will thank you aloud as I offer my sacrifice.  
> I will keep the promise I made.  
> The Lord is the one who saves.

The Lord spoke to the fish. It vomited Jonah onto dry land.

*Original Scripture [Jonah 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/JON02.htm).*

## Chapter 3 — Forty Days

The Lord spoke to Jonah a second time. “Get up. Go to Nineveh, that great city, and announce the message I give you.”

This time Jonah went as the Lord had told him. Nineveh was enormous, a city that took three days to cross. Jonah went a day’s journey into it, calling out a warning: “Forty more days, and Nineveh will fall!”

The people believed God. Word went out that everyone should fast. People of every rank put on sackcloth. The warning reached the king. He stood up from his throne, took off his royal robe, wrapped himself in sackcloth, and sat down in ashes.

The king and his nobles sent an order through Nineveh. No person or animal, no herd or flock, was to taste food or drink water. People and animals were to be covered in sackcloth. The people were to cry out to God with all their strength. Each of them was to turn away from evil and put an end to the violence they had been doing.

Perhaps God would relent. Perhaps his fierce anger would turn away, and they would live.

God saw what they did. He saw them turn from their evil ways. He held back the disaster he had said would come upon them.

*Original Scripture [Jonah 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/JON03.htm).*

## Chapter 4 — A Little Shade

Jonah was furious. He took his anger to the Lord.

“Isn’t this what I said back in my own country? This is why I hurried away to Tarshish. I knew you were gracious and merciful, slow to grow angry and full of faithful love. I knew you would hold back from bringing disaster. Now, Lord, please take my life. I’d rather die than live.”

The Lord asked him whether he was right to be angry.

Jonah went east of the city. There he built a shelter and sat in its shade to see what would happen to Nineveh. The Lord God made a vine grow up over Jonah. Its shade fell across his head and gave him relief. Jonah was delighted with the vine.

At dawn the next day, God sent a worm to chew through it. The vine withered. When the sun rose, God sent a hot east wind. The sun beat down on Jonah’s head until he felt faint. He asked to die. Again he said that death would be better than life.

God asked whether he was right to be angry about the vine.

“Yes,” Jonah said. “Angry enough to die.”

The Lord pointed out that Jonah cared about a vine he had never tended or helped to grow. It had sprung up in a night and died in a night. And what about Nineveh, a great city with more than 120,000 people who could not tell their right hand from their left, and with many animals besides? Should he have no concern for them?

*Original Scripture [Jonah 4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/JON04.htm).*
