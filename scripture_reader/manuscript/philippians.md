# Philippians

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold - A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The public-domain Protestant World English Bible is the source. Read this adaptation alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 — The Work God Began

Paul and Timothy wrote to all God's holy people in Philippi, including overseers and servants, with grace and peace.

Paul thanked God joyfully for their partnership in the good news. The God who began a good work among them would complete it at Christ's day. They shared grace with Paul in imprisonment and in defending the gospel. He prayed that their love would overflow with knowledge and discernment, producing righteous fruit.

His chains had advanced the message: the palace guard knew he was imprisoned for Christ, and believers spoke more boldly. Some preached from love, others from envy and selfish ambition. Paul rejoiced that Christ was proclaimed.

Through their prayers and Christ's Spirit, he expected deliverance and courage. To live was Christ and to die was gain. Departure to be with Christ was better, but remaining meant fruitful service and helped their faith and joy.

Whatever happened, they must live as citizens worthy of the gospel, standing in one spirit and struggling together without fear. They had received both faith in Christ and suffering for him, sharing Paul's conflict.

*Original Scripture [Philippians 1](https://ebible.org/engwebp/PHP01.htm).*

## Chapter 2 — The Mind of Christ

Christ's encouragement, love, Spirit-fellowship, and compassion should make them united in love and purpose. They must reject selfish ambition, value others humbly, and consider one another's interests.

They should share Christ's mind. Though in God's form, he did not exploit equality with God. He emptied himself, took a servant's form, became human, and obeyed to death on a cross. God therefore exalted him and gave him the highest name, so every knee will bow and every tongue confess Jesus Christ as Lord.

They should obey and work out their salvation reverently, because God worked within their desire and action. Without complaining or arguing, they could shine like lights while holding the word of life. Even if Paul were poured out like an offering upon their faith, he would rejoice.

Paul hoped to send Timothy, whose genuine concern and proven service were rare, and trusted he could also visit.

He was sending Epaphroditus, their brother, coworker, fellow soldier, messenger, and minister. Epaphroditus had nearly died for Christ's work, but God showed mercy. They should welcome and honor him for risking his life to supply the service distance prevented them from giving.

*Original Scripture [Philippians 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/PHP02.htm).*

## Chapter 3 — Pressing toward the Goal

They should rejoice in the Lord and beware of corrupt workers who treated circumcision as the ground of belonging. God's people worship by the Spirit, rejoice in Christ, and trust no human qualifications.

Paul could have boasted: circumcised on the eighth day, Israelite, Benjaminite, Hebrew, Pharisee, zealous persecutor, and outwardly blameless. Yet he counted every gain as loss beside knowing Christ. He wanted righteousness through faith, resurrection power, fellowship in Christ's sufferings, and likeness to his death.

He had not reached perfection. Forgetting what lay behind, he pressed toward the prize of God's upward call. Mature believers should think this way and walk consistently with their progress.

They should imitate faithful examples. Paul wept over enemies of the cross whose appetite was their god, whose glory was shame, and whose minds stayed earthly.

Their citizenship was in heaven. From there they awaited Jesus Christ, who would transform their weak bodies to resemble his glorious body by the power that subjects all things to him.

*Original Scripture [Philippians 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/PHP03.htm).*

## Chapter 4 — Joy, Peace, and a Fragrant Gift

Paul called them his joy and crown and urged them to stand firm. Euodia and Syntyche should agree in the Lord, helped by a loyal coworker; they had struggled beside Paul, Clement, and others whose names were in the book of life.

They should rejoice always, show gentleness, and remember the Lord was near. Instead of anxiety, they should bring requests with thanksgiving. God's surpassing peace would guard their hearts and minds.

Their thoughts should dwell on whatever was true, honorable, just, pure, lovely, admirable, excellent, and praiseworthy. They should practice what Paul taught and modeled.

Paul rejoiced that their concern had blossomed again. He had learned contentment in hunger or fullness, poverty or abundance; through the one strengthening him, he could face every condition.

They had shared his trouble. From the gospel's beginning in Macedonia, they alone partnered in giving and receiving, sending help even in Thessalonica. Paul sought not profit but fruit credited to them. Epaphroditus brought a fragrant, acceptable sacrifice.

God would supply their needs through Christ. Paul greeted every saint, especially those of Caesar's household. May Christ's grace be with their spirit.

*Original Scripture [Philippians 4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/PHP04.htm).*

| discernment | 1 | Wise judgment recognizing what truly matters. |
| palace guard | 1 | Soldiers attached to Roman governing authority. |
| citizenship | 1 | Membership carrying identity, duties, and privileges. |
| humility | 2 | Freedom from pride that values and serves others. |
| exploit | 2 | Use a position or advantage selfishly. |
| drink offering | 2 | Liquid poured in worship; here, a life freely given. |
| circumcision | 3 | Israel's covenant sign, wrongly treated here as a basis for salvation. |
| Pharisee | 3 | A member of a Jewish movement strict about religious law. |
