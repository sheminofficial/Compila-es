# Obadiah

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold — A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The Protestant World English Bible is the public-domain source. Prophetic imagery is preserved without claiming more certainty than the text provides. This adaptation is intended to be read alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 — From the Heights to the Dust

Obadiah’s vision opened with a message about Edom. News had gone out from the Lord, and a messenger was moving among the nations: they were to rise and prepare for battle against her.

Edom would be made small among the nations and treated with contempt. The people had built homes among rocky cliffs and lived high above the ground. From those heights, safety seemed certain. Pride whispered that no one could bring them down.

But height was not the same as security before God.

Even if Edom rose like an eagle, even if its nest seemed to rest among the stars, the Lord said he would bring it down.

Ordinary thieves stop when they have stolen enough. Workers gathering grapes leave at least a few behind. The disaster coming to Edom would be more complete. Esau—the ancestor whose name represented the nation—would be searched and stripped. Treasures hidden away from sight would be discovered.

The allies Edom trusted would drive its people as far as the border. Those who had spoken of peace would deceive and overpower them. Friends who had eaten at their table would secretly set a trap beneath them, while Edom failed to understand what was happening.

On that day, the Lord would remove the wise from Edom and understanding from the mountain country of Esau. Teman’s warriors would lose their courage. Violence would cut people off from the mountain.

The reason for the judgment was not hidden. Edom had acted violently against Jacob, the brother nation descended from Esau’s twin. Shame would now cover Edom, and its power would be cut off.

When foreigners entered Jerusalem, carried away its wealth, and cast lots over the city, Edom had stood apart. Yet standing aside did not make the nation innocent. By watching without compassion, Edom had become like the invaders.

Obadiah’s warning repeated the day of disaster like the beat of a drum.

Do not look down on your brother when calamity comes. Do not celebrate when Judah is destroyed. Do not speak proudly while others are in distress. Do not enter the city gate to take advantage of the ruin. Do not seize wealth from suffering people. Do not wait at the crossroads to capture those who escape, and do not hand survivors over to their enemies.

The day of the Lord was near—not only for Edom, but for all nations. What Edom had done would be done to Edom. Its own actions would return upon its head.

The prophet pictured nations drinking judgment. As Edom had joined the violence on God’s holy mountain, the nations would drink in turn. They would swallow until their proud place disappeared.

Yet Mount Zion would not remain empty forever. Survivors would be there, and the mountain would be holy. Jacob’s family would again possess an inheritance.

Then the vision became fire. The house of Jacob would be a blaze, and the house of Joseph a flame. The house of Esau would be dry straw before them. The flame would spread through the stubble until none of Edom’s power remained. Obadiah did not present this as a rumor or a wish: the Lord had spoken.

The closing lines moved across a map of restoration. People from the south would possess Esau’s mountain. Those from the western foothills would possess Philistine territory, along with the fields of Ephraim and Samaria. Benjamin would possess Gilead. Israelite exiles among the Canaanites would receive land as far as Zarephath, and exiles from Jerusalem in Sepharad would possess towns in the Negev.

Deliverers would go up Mount Zion to judge the mountains of Esau.

Edom’s cliffs, alliances, treasure, wisdom, and soldiers had all appeared strong. The final sentence placed every human kingdom beneath a greater authority:

The kingdom would belong to the Lord.

## Glossary candidates

| Expression | First chapter | Meaning in context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| contempt | 1 | The feeling that someone is unimportant or deserves no respect. |
| cleft | 1 | A narrow opening or split in a rock. |
| ransack | 1 | Search a place roughly and take things from it. |
| alliance | 1 | An agreement between people or nations to support one another. |
| set a trap | 1 | Secretly prepare something intended to catch or defeat someone. |
| calamity | 1 | A terrible event that causes great loss or suffering. |
| cast lots | 1 | Use marked objects and chance to make a choice or divide something. |
| seize | 1 | Take something suddenly or by force. |
| stubble | 1 | Short, dry plant stems left in a field after harvest. |
| foothills | 1 | Low hills near the base of mountains. |
