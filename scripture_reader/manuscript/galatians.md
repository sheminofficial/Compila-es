# Galatians

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold - A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The public-domain Protestant World English Bible is the source. Read this adaptation alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 — No Other Good News

Paul, an apostle sent not by humans but by Jesus Christ and the Father who raised him, wrote with the believers beside him to Galatia. Jesus gave himself for sins to rescue people from the present evil age.

Paul was astonished that they were quickly deserting grace for a distorted message. There was no other gospel. Even an angel preaching contrary to what they received should be rejected. Paul sought God's approval, not human applause.

His message was not invented or taught by people but revealed by Jesus. They knew his former life in Judaism: he persecuted God's church and advanced beyond his peers, zealous for ancestral traditions.

But God, who set him apart before birth, revealed his Son so Paul could proclaim him among nations. Paul did not immediately consult human leaders or travel to Jerusalem; he went to Arabia, returned to Damascus, and after three years visited Cephas for fifteen days, also seeing James, the Lord's brother.

He later went to Syria and Cilicia. Judean churches knew only that their former persecutor now preached the faith he once destroyed, and they glorified God.

*Original Scripture [Galatians 1](https://ebible.org/engwebp/GAL01.htm).*

## Chapter 2 — Justified by Faith

After fourteen years Paul returned to Jerusalem with Barnabas and Titus, privately presenting his gospel to recognized leaders. Though Titus was Greek, he was not forced to be circumcised. False brothers had slipped in to spy on freedom, but Paul yielded nothing so gospel truth would remain.

James, Cephas, and John recognized grace given to Paul and extended fellowship to him and Barnabas: they would serve the nations while the others served the circumcised. They asked only remembrance of the poor.

At Antioch, Paul opposed Cephas publicly. Cephas had eaten with Gentiles but withdrew when people from James arrived, fearing the circumcision group. Other Jews, even Barnabas, joined the hypocrisy.

Paul said Jews and Gentiles alike are justified not by law's works but through faith in Jesus Christ. If righteousness came through law, Christ died for nothing.

Through law Paul died to law so he could live to God. He had been crucified with Christ; Christ now lived in him. His bodily life was lived by faith in God's Son, who loved him and gave himself for him.

*Original Scripture [Galatians 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/GAL02.htm).*

## Chapter 3 — The Promise Given to Abraham

The Galatians had seen Christ crucified clearly proclaimed. Did they receive the Spirit by law works or hearing with faith? Having begun by the Spirit, could human effort complete them? God supplied the Spirit and miracles through faith.

Abraham believed God and it was counted as righteousness. Those of faith were Abraham's children. Scripture foresaw nations justified by faith and announced beforehand that all nations would be blessed through Abraham.

Those relying on law works stood under its curse because no one performed everything written. Christ redeemed them from the curse by becoming a curse on the cross, so Abraham's blessing and the promised Spirit could reach nations.

A later law could not cancel God's earlier promise. The inheritance rested on promise. The law was added because of transgressions until the promised offspring came, and acted as a guardian leading toward Christ.

Now faith had come; believers were God's children through faith. Those baptized into Christ had put on Christ. Jew and Greek, enslaved and free, male and female were one in Christ. Belonging to Christ made them Abraham's offspring and heirs by promise.

*Original Scripture [Galatians 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/GAL03.htm).*

## Chapter 4 — Children, Not Slaves

An underage heir resembles a servant though owning everything, remaining under guardians until the father's time. Likewise people were enslaved under elemental principles.

When time was full, God sent his Son, born of woman and under law, to redeem those under law so they could receive adoption. Because they were children, God sent his Son's Spirit into their hearts crying, "Abba, Father." They were no longer slaves but heirs.

Once they served beings not truly gods. Now known by God, why return to weak principles and observe days, months, seasons, and years as bondage?

Paul begged them to become like him. They had not wronged him. Though bodily illness first brought him to them, they welcomed him like an angel, even like Christ. False teachers courted them to isolate them.

Paul felt childbirth pains until Christ formed in them and wished he could change his tone.

Abraham had sons by Hagar the enslaved woman and Sarah the free woman. Paul used them as an allegory: Hagar corresponded to Sinai and present slavery; the Jerusalem above was free and their mother. Like Isaac, believers were children of promise. The enslaved woman's son persecuted the free woman's son, but the inheritance belonged to freedom.

*Original Scripture [Galatians 4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/GAL04.htm).*

## Chapter 5 — The Fruit of the Spirit

Christ set them free, so they must stand firm and refuse slavery's yoke. Receiving circumcision as a basis for righteousness obligated a person to the whole law and severed him from relying on Christ's grace.

Through the Spirit, by faith, believers awaited righteousness's hope. In Christ neither circumcision nor uncircumcision mattered, but faith working through love.

They had been running well. The persuasion blocking truth did not come from God. A little yeast affected the whole batch. Paul wished the agitators would remove themselves.

Freedom was not permission for selfish desire but a call to serve through love. The whole law was fulfilled in loving one's neighbor. Biting and devouring would destroy them.

They should walk by the Spirit rather than satisfy fleshly desire. Flesh and Spirit opposed each other. The flesh's works included immorality, idolatry, hatred, jealousy, rage, factions, envy, drunkenness, and similar things.

The Spirit's fruit was love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. Those belonging to Christ crucified the flesh with its passions. If they lived by the Spirit, they should walk by the Spirit, without pride, provocation, or envy.

*Original Scripture [Galatians 5](https://ebible.org/engwebp/GAL05.htm).*

## Chapter 6 — Carry One Another's Burdens

Spiritual believers should restore someone caught in wrongdoing gently while watching themselves. They should carry one another's burdens and fulfill Christ's law, yet each person must examine and carry personal responsibility. Learners should share good things with teachers.

God cannot be mocked: people harvest what they plant. Sowing to selfish flesh brings decay; sowing to the Spirit brings eternal life. They must not grow tired of doing good, for harvest would come in season. While opportunity remained, they should do good to everyone, especially faith's household.

Paul wrote the final lines in large letters with his own hand. Those demanding circumcision wanted outward respect and to avoid persecution for Christ's cross. They did not keep law themselves but wanted to boast in others' flesh.

Paul would boast only in Jesus Christ's cross, through which the world was crucified to him and he to the world. Neither circumcision nor uncircumcision mattered, but new creation.

Peace and mercy belonged to those following this rule and to God's Israel. No one should trouble Paul, for he carried Jesus' scars on his body.

May Jesus Christ's grace be with their spirit.

*Original Scripture [Galatians 6](https://ebible.org/engwebp/GAL06.htm).*
