# 2 Timothy

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold — A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The Protestant World English Bible is the public-domain source. The letter’s personal names, prison setting, warnings, ministry commands, and final travel details are preserved. This adaptation is intended to be read alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 — Guard What Was Entrusted to You

Paul wrote as an apostle of Jesus Christ by God’s will, serving the promise of life found in Christ. His letter went to Timothy, his beloved child in the faith.

Grace, mercy, and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus their Lord.

Night and day, Timothy returned to Paul’s prayers. Paul served God with a clear conscience, as his ancestors had, and he longed to see his friend again. He remembered Timothy’s tears. A reunion would fill him with joy.

He also remembered Timothy’s sincere faith. It had first lived in Timothy’s grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice. Paul was convinced that the same faith lived in Timothy.

For that reason, Timothy should stir God’s gift into flame—the gift connected with Paul’s laying his hands upon him. God had not given them a spirit that shrank back in fear, but one of power, love, and self-control.

Timothy must not be ashamed of the testimony about Jesus or of Paul, now a prisoner for the Lord. Instead, he should share in suffering for the good news, relying on God’s power.

God had saved and called them to a holy life, not because their works earned it, but because of his own purpose and grace. This grace had been given in Christ before the ages and had now appeared through their Savior. Jesus Christ had broken death’s power and brought life and immortality into the light through the gospel.

Paul had been appointed its herald, apostle, and teacher. That calling explained his suffering. Still, he was not ashamed. He knew the one he trusted and was persuaded that God could guard what Paul had entrusted to him until the final day.

Timothy should preserve the pattern of healthy teaching heard from Paul, holding it with faith and love in Christ. The Holy Spirit living within them would help him guard the good deposit placed in his care.

Paul knew the pain of abandonment. People throughout the province of Asia had turned away from him, including Phygelus and Hermogenes.

One household had acted differently.

Paul prayed for mercy upon the family of Onesiphorus. Onesiphorus had often refreshed him and had not been ashamed of his chains. When he reached Rome, he searched diligently until he found Paul. Timothy already knew how generously he had served in Ephesus.

May the Lord allow Onesiphorus to receive mercy on that day.

Timothy inherited more than teaching. He inherited a choice between shame and courage, abandonment and loyal presence. He was to fan his gift into flame, endure suffering, and guard the message through the Spirit who remained near even when human friends left.

## Chapter 2 — The Word Is Not Chained

Paul called Timothy his child and urged him to draw strength from the grace found in Christ Jesus.

What Timothy had heard from Paul in the presence of many witnesses must not stop with him. He should entrust it to faithful people capable of teaching others. The message would move from one life into another, generation after generation.

That work required endurance. Paul offered three pictures.

A good soldier accepts hardship and avoids becoming tangled in ordinary affairs because he wants to please the commander who enlisted him. An athlete receives the victor’s crown only by competing according to the rules. A hardworking farmer should be the first to share the crop.

Timothy was to think carefully about these images, trusting the Lord to give understanding.

Above every metaphor stood the central memory: Jesus Christ, raised from the dead and descended from David. This was the good news Paul preached.

For that message Paul suffered in chains like a criminal. But God’s word was not chained.

He endured for the sake of God’s chosen people so that they too might receive salvation in Christ with eternal glory.

A trustworthy saying gathered the promise and warning: if believers died with Christ, they would live with him. If they endured, they would reign with him. If they denied him, he would deny them. If they became faithless, he remained faithful, because he could not deny his own character.

Timothy should remind the church of these things and warn them before God not to fight over words. Such arguments produced no good and ruined those who listened.

Timothy himself must work diligently to stand approved before God, like a worker with no reason for shame, handling the word of truth correctly. Empty religious chatter would spread ungodliness like gangrene.

Hymenaeus and Philetus offered an example. They had wandered from truth by claiming the resurrection had already happened, overturning some people’s faith.

Yet God’s foundation remained firm, carrying a double inscription: the Lord knew those who belonged to him, and everyone who named the Lord must turn away from injustice.

A large house held vessels made of gold and silver as well as wood and clay. Some were reserved for honorable purposes, others for ordinary or dishonorable use. A person who cleansed himself from what was corrupt could become a vessel set apart, useful to the Master and prepared for every good work.

Timothy must run from youthful desires and pursue righteousness, faith, love, and peace alongside people who called on the Lord from pure hearts.

He should refuse foolish questions that produced fights. The Lord’s servant could not be quarrelsome. He must be kind to everyone, skilled at teaching, patient when wronged, and gentle when correcting opponents.

Perhaps God would grant those opponents repentance and a full knowledge of truth. They might recover their senses and escape the devil’s trap after being captured to do his will.

The goal of correction was not victory in an argument. It was rescue. The teacher who knew the word was unchained must not let his own temper place another chain around it.

## Chapter 3 — Equipped for Every Good Work

Paul warned that the last days would include difficult seasons.

People would turn love inward. They would love themselves, money, and pleasure more than God. Boasting and arrogance would fill their speech. They would insult what was holy, disobey parents, reject gratitude, and lose reverence.

Natural affection would fade. People would refuse reconciliation, spread slander, lose self-control, become brutal, and hate goodness. They would betray others, rush forward recklessly, and swell with pride.

They might preserve the outward shape of religion while denying its power. Timothy was to turn away from such influence.

Some entered households quietly and captured vulnerable women already weighed down by guilt and driven by many desires. These victims were always receiving new information but never reaching knowledge of truth.

Paul compared the deceivers to Jannes and Jambres, names associated with the men who opposed Moses. Their minds were corrupted, and their claim to faith had failed the test. Their progress would not continue forever. Their foolishness would become visible, just as the folly of Moses’s opponents had.

Timothy had followed a different pattern. He knew Paul’s teaching, conduct, purpose, faith, patience, love, and endurance. He also knew the persecutions and sufferings Paul faced at Antioch, Iconium, and Lystra. Paul had endured them, and the Lord had rescued him from them all.

Everyone desiring a godly life in Christ Jesus should expect persecution. Meanwhile evil people and impostors would move from bad to worse, deceiving others while being deceived themselves.

Timothy must remain in what he had learned and come to trust, remembering the people from whom he learned it. From infancy he had known the sacred writings. Those Scriptures could make him wise for salvation through faith in Jesus Christ.

Every Scripture is breathed out by God. Therefore it is useful: it teaches truth, exposes wrong, corrects a wandering path, and trains people to live rightly.

Its purpose was not merely to fill a mind with information. Scripture prepared the person who belonged to God to become complete and thoroughly equipped for every good work.

The contrast ran through the chapter. Some people were always learning without reaching truth. Timothy had received Scripture from faithful people and was to remain in it until truth shaped conduct, endurance, love, and useful action.

## Chapter 4 — Bring the Cloak before Winter

Paul gave Timothy a solemn command in the presence of God and Jesus Christ, who would judge the living and the dead when he appeared in his kingdom.

Preach the word. Be ready when the moment feels convenient and when it does not. Correct, rebuke, and encourage—with complete patience and careful teaching.

A time would come when people refused healthy doctrine. Their ears would itch for messages matching their desires, so they would gather teachers who told them what they wanted to hear. They would turn away from truth and wander into myths.

Timothy must remain clear-minded. He should endure hardship, do the work of an evangelist, and fulfill his ministry completely.

Paul’s own ministry was reaching its end. His life was already being poured out like an offering, and the time of departure had arrived.

He had fought the good fight. He had finished the race. He had kept the faith.

A crown of righteousness waited for him. The Lord, the righteous judge, would award it on that day—not only to Paul, but to everyone who loved Christ’s appearing.

Then the grand language gave way to requests from a lonely prisoner.

“Do your best to come to me soon.”

Demas had loved the present world and left for Thessalonica. Crescens had gone to Galatia, and Titus to Dalmatia. Only Luke remained with Paul.

Timothy should bring Mark because Mark had become useful to Paul’s service. Tychicus had been sent to Ephesus.

Paul also wanted the cloak left with Carpus in Troas. Winter was approaching. Timothy should bring the books too—especially the parchments.

Alexander the metalworker had caused Paul great harm and strongly opposed the message. The Lord would repay him according to his actions. Timothy should remain on guard.

At Paul’s first defense, no human supporter stood beside him. Everyone left. Paul prayed that their abandonment would not be counted against them.

The Lord, however, stood with him and gave him strength. Paul was able to proclaim the message fully so that the Gentiles heard, and he was rescued from the lion’s mouth.

He trusted the Lord to rescue him from every evil work and bring him safely into the heavenly kingdom. Glory belonged to God forever.

Paul sent greetings to Prisca, Aquila, and the household of Onesiphorus. Erastus had remained in Corinth. Trophimus had been left sick in Miletus—a quiet detail showing that faithful servants could still experience illness.

Again Paul urged Timothy to arrive before winter.

Eubulus, Pudens, Linus, Claudia, and all the brothers sent greetings.

May the Lord be with Timothy’s spirit. Grace be with them all.

The final scene held both confidence and ordinary need: a faithful apostle awaiting a crown, and an aging prisoner asking a beloved friend to hurry, bring Mark, remember the books, and not forget his cloak.

*Original Scripture [2 Timothy 4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/2TI04.htm).*
