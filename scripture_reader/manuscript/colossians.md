# Colossians

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold - A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The public-domain Protestant World English Bible is the source. This retelling preserves the letter's people, places, arguments, warnings, household teaching, and greetings. Read it alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 - The Son Who Holds All Things Together

Paul, an apostle by God's will, and Timothy wrote to the faithful believers in Colossae with grace and peace.

They thanked God for the Colossians' faith in Christ, love for God's people, and hope stored in heaven. The true good news was bearing fruit throughout the world, as it had among them since Epaphras, their beloved fellow servant, taught them God's grace and reported their Spirit-given love.

Paul prayed that they would know God's will with spiritual wisdom, live worthily, bear fruit, grow in knowing God, and endure with joyful patience. The Father had qualified them for the saints' inheritance in light, rescued them from darkness, and transferred them into his beloved Son's kingdom, where they had redemption and forgiveness.

The Son is the image of the invisible God and firstborn over creation. All visible and invisible things - thrones, dominions, rulers, and authorities - were created through him and for him. He existed before everything, and in him everything holds together. He is the head of the church, his body, and the firstborn from the dead.

God's fullness was pleased to live in him and reconcile all things through his cross-blood. Once alienated and hostile, the Colossians had been reconciled through Christ's bodily death to stand holy and blameless, if they remained grounded in gospel hope.

Paul suffered for Christ's body, the church. He was commissioned to reveal the mystery hidden for ages but now made known among the nations: Christ in them, the hope of glory. He warned and taught everyone with wisdom, laboring by God's power so each person might become mature in Christ.

*Original Scripture [Colossians 1](https://ebible.org/engwebp/COL01.htm).*

## Chapter 2 - Rooted in Christ

Paul struggled for Colossae, Laodicea, and believers who had never met him. He wanted their hearts encouraged and joined in love, knowing God's mystery, Christ, in whom every treasure of wisdom and knowledge is hidden. Persuasive speech must not deceive them.

Having received Christ Jesus as Lord, they should walk in him: rooted, built up, established in faith, and overflowing with gratitude. Empty philosophy based on human tradition and the world's principles must not capture them.

All the fullness of deity lives bodily in Christ, and believers are complete in him, the head of every authority. In him they received a circumcision not made by hands. Buried with him in baptism, they were raised through faith in God's power. Though dead in wrongdoing, God made them alive with Christ and forgave them.

The written debt against them was erased and nailed to the cross. Christ disarmed rulers and authorities and triumphed over them.

No one should condemn them over food, drink, festivals, new moons, or Sabbaths. These were shadows; the reality belongs to Christ. Nor should false humility, angel worship, visions, and pride disqualify them. Such teaching loses connection with Christ the Head, from whom the whole body grows.

Since they died with Christ to the world's principles, why submit to "Do not handle, taste, or touch"? These human commands concern perishable things. They appear wise through invented worship, humility, and harsh bodily treatment, but cannot conquer selfish desire.

*Original Scripture [Colossians 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/COL02.htm).*

## Chapter 3 - Put On Love

Since believers were raised with Christ, they should seek what is above, where Christ sits at God's right hand. They had died, and their life was hidden with Christ in God. When Christ appeared, they would appear with him in glory.

They must put to death immorality, impurity, uncontrolled passion, evil desire, and greed, which is idolatry. They once walked that way, but now must discard anger, rage, malice, slander, filthy speech, and lies.

They had removed the old self and put on the new, renewed according to the Creator's image. Greek and Jew, circumcised and uncircumcised, foreigner, Scythian, enslaved and free could not create rank there: Christ was all and in all.

As God's chosen and beloved people, they should clothe themselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience. They must bear with one another, forgive as the Lord forgave, and put on love, the bond holding everything together.

Christ's peace should rule in their one body. His word should live richly among them as they taught wisely and sang psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs with gratitude. Every word and action should be done in Jesus' name with thanks to the Father.

Wives should submit as fitting in the Lord. Husbands must love without harshness. Children should obey; fathers must not provoke them into discouragement. Enslaved people were told to obey earthly masters sincerely, not merely while watched, working wholeheartedly for the Lord. Christ was their true Master and would give the inheritance; wrongdoing would meet impartial justice.

*Original Scripture [Colossians 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/COL03.htm).*

## Chapter 4 - Speech Seasoned with Grace

Masters must provide what is just and fair, remembering their Master in heaven.

The church should remain alert and thankful in prayer. They should ask God to open a door for Paul to speak Christ's mystery clearly even in chains. Toward outsiders, they should act wisely and use every opportunity. Their speech should carry grace, seasoned with salt, so they knew how to answer each person.

Tychicus, Paul's beloved brother, faithful minister, and fellow servant, would explain Paul's circumstances and encourage them. Onesimus, their faithful and beloved brother from Colossae, would accompany him.

Aristarchus, Paul's fellow prisoner, sent greetings, as did Mark, Barnabas's cousin, whom they should welcome, and Jesus called Justus. These Jewish coworkers for God's kingdom had comforted Paul.

Epaphras, one of their own and Christ's servant, always struggled in prayer that they might stand mature and complete in God's will. Paul testified to his hard work for Colossae, Laodicea, and Hierapolis. Luke, the beloved physician, and Demas also greeted them.

Paul greeted the Laodicean believers, Nymphas, and the church in that house. The letter should be exchanged and publicly read with the church in Laodicea. Archippus must complete the ministry received in the Lord.

Paul added the greeting in his own hand: remember his chains. Grace be with them.

*Original Scripture [Colossians 4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/COL04.htm).*
