# 1 Timothy

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold - A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The public-domain Protestant World English Bible is the source. Read this adaptation alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 @@ Grace for the Chief of Sinners

Paul, an apostle by God's command, wrote to Timothy, his true child in faith, with grace, mercy, and peace.

Timothy must remain in Ephesus and stop certain teachers from spreading different doctrine, myths, and endless genealogies. Such speculation produced arguments rather than God's work through faith. The goal of instruction was love from a pure heart, clear conscience, and sincere faith.

Some had wandered into empty talk and wanted to teach law without understanding it. Law was good when used lawfully, confronting whatever opposed healthy teaching and the glorious gospel entrusted to Paul.

Paul thanked Christ for strengthening and appointing him, though formerly a blasphemer, persecutor, and violent man. Mercy met his ignorant unbelief, and grace overflowed with faith and love. Christ came to save sinners, of whom Paul called himself foremost. His mercy displayed Jesus' perfect patience as an example for future believers.

To the eternal, immortal, invisible, only God belonged honor and glory.

Paul entrusted Timothy with this charge according to earlier prophecies: fight the good fight, holding faith and a good conscience. Hymenaeus and Alexander rejected conscience and wrecked their faith; Paul handed them over for discipline so they might learn not to blaspheme.

*Original Scripture [1 Timothy 1](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1TI01.htm).*

## Chapter 2 @@ Prayer for All People

First of all, believers should offer requests, prayers, intercessions, and thanksgiving for everyone, including kings and authorities, so they could live peaceful, reverent lives.

This pleased God, who desires people to be saved and know truth. There is one God and one mediator between God and humanity, Christ Jesus, who gave himself as ransom for all. Paul was appointed herald, apostle, and teacher of the nations.

Men should pray everywhere with holy hands, without anger or arguing.

Women should dress modestly and sensibly, adorning themselves with good works rather than costly display. They should learn quietly and submissively. Paul grounded his restriction on teaching authority in Adam and Eve's creation and deception, then spoke of preservation through childbearing as women continued in faith, love, holiness, and self-control.

*Original Scripture [1 Timothy 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1TI02.htm).*

## Chapter 3 @@ Care for God's Household

Anyone aspiring to oversee a church desired noble work. An overseer must be above reproach, faithful in marriage, sober, self-controlled, respectable, hospitable, able to teach, not violent or addicted to wine, but gentle, peaceful, and free from love of money.

He must manage his household well, for inability there raised questions about caring for God's church. He could not be a recent convert, lest pride bring the devil's condemnation. Outsiders should respect him so he avoided disgrace and a snare.

Servants likewise must be dignified, truthful, sober, and not greedy, holding faith's mystery with clear conscience. They should first be tested. Women likewise must be dignified, not slanderers, sober, and faithful. Faithful service brought good standing and confidence in Christ.

Paul hoped to visit but wrote so Timothy knew how people should behave in God's household, the church of the living God, pillar and foundation of truth.

The revealed mystery was great: Christ appeared in flesh, was vindicated by the Spirit, seen by angels, proclaimed among nations, believed in the world, and taken up in glory.

*Original Scripture [1 Timothy 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1TI03.htm).*

## Chapter 4 @@ Train Yourself in Godliness

The Spirit warned that later some would abandon faith for deceiving spirits and demonic teachings, promoted by hypocritical liars with seared consciences. They forbade marriage and foods God created to be received thankfully. God's creation is good and made holy through word and prayer.

Timothy should teach these truths as a good servant nourished by faith, rejecting irreverent myths. Physical training had limited value, but godliness held promise for present and future life. They labored because hope rested in the living God, Savior of all people, especially believers.

No one should despise Timothy's youth. He must model speech, conduct, love, faith, and purity. Until Paul came, he should devote himself to public Scripture reading, exhortation, and teaching.

He must not neglect his gift, given through prophecy with elders laying hands upon him. Practicing these things visibly, watching life and teaching, and persevering would help both himself and his hearers.

*Original Scripture [1 Timothy 4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1TI04.htm).*

## Chapter 5 @@ Honor and Wise Care

Timothy should not harshly rebuke an older man but appeal as to a father; younger men as brothers, older women as mothers, and younger women as sisters in purity.

The church should honor widows truly alone. Families must first repay parents and grandparents. A genuine widow hoped in God and prayed continually, while self-indulgence brought spiritual death.

A widow enrolled for ongoing support should be at least sixty, faithful in marriage, and known for raising children, hospitality, serving believers, helping afflicted people, and every good work. Younger widows should remarry and manage households rather than become idle gossips. Anyone with widows in the family must care for them so the church could help those truly alone.

Elders leading well deserved double honor, especially preachers and teachers. Scripture said not to muzzle an ox and that a worker deserved wages. An accusation against an elder required two or three witnesses; persistent sin required public correction.

Timothy must act without prejudice or favoritism, avoid hasty appointment and participation in others' sins, and keep pure. Because of frequent illness, he should use a little wine rather than water alone. Some sins and good works appeared early; others became visible later.

*Original Scripture [1 Timothy 5](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1TI05.htm).*

## Chapter 6 @@ Fight the Good Fight

Enslaved believers should respect masters so God's name was not slandered; believing masters should not be treated carelessly but served as beloved fellow believers.

False teachers rejected Jesus' healthy words and godly teaching. Pride produced controversies, envy, strife, slander, suspicion, and conflict among people treating godliness as profit.

Godliness with contentment is great gain. People brought nothing into the world and take nothing out. Food and clothing should satisfy. Those determined to be rich fall into traps and harmful desires. Love of money is a root of many evils and has led some from faith into sorrow.

Timothy, God's man, must flee these things and pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance, and gentleness. He should fight faith's good fight and grasp eternal life, keeping the command blameless until Jesus appears.

God, the blessed Sovereign, King of kings and Lord of lords, alone possesses immortality and dwells in unapproachable light.

The rich should not be arrogant or trust uncertain wealth, but God, who richly provides enjoyment. They should be rich in good works, generous, and ready to share, storing a firm foundation for true life.

Timothy must guard the entrusted deposit, avoiding irreverent chatter and falsely named knowledge that caused some to miss faith.

Grace be with them.

*Original Scripture [1 Timothy 6](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1TI06.htm).*
