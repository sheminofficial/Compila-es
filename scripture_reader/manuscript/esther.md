# Esther

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold - A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The public-domain Protestant World English Bible is the source. This adaptation preserves the Persian court setting, narrative reversals, names, decrees, dates, and the book's deliberate lack of explicit reference to God. Read it alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 — A Queen Refuses

King Ahasuerus ruled 127 provinces from India to Ethiopia. In his third year he displayed Persia and Media's wealth to officials for 180 days, then gave a seven-day feast in Susa's garden court. Rich hangings, marble pillars, golden couches, and varied golden cups surrounded unlimited royal wine. Queen Vashti held a feast for women.

On the seventh day, merry with wine, the king ordered seven eunuchs to bring Vashti wearing her crown so guests could admire her beauty. She refused. The king burned with anger and consulted seven wise princes.

Memucan argued Vashti had wronged not only the king but every husband; women hearing her refusal would despise husbands. He proposed an irrevocable Persian decree removing Vashti and giving her position to someone better, so wives would honor husbands.

The king agreed. Letters went to every province in its language declaring each man ruler in his household.

*Original Scripture [Esther 1](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EST01.htm).*

## Chapter 2 — Esther Becomes Queen

After anger cooled, attendants proposed gathering beautiful young virgins into Susa under Hegai's care so the king could choose a queen.

Mordecai, a Benjaminite descended from Kish's exiles, lived in Susa. He had raised his orphaned cousin Hadassah, called Esther. Esther was taken to the palace, pleased Hegai, and received beauty treatments, food, seven attendants, and the best quarters. At Mordecai's instruction she hid her Jewish identity.

Each woman prepared twelve months before appearing: six with myrrh oil and six with perfumes. Esther requested only what Hegai advised and won favor.

In the seventh year, the king loved Esther above the others, crowned her queen, and held a great feast. She still obeyed Mordecai and concealed her people.

While Mordecai sat at the gate, Bigthan and Teresh plotted to kill the king. Mordecai told Esther, who reported it in his name. Investigation proved the plot; both men were hanged, and the event entered the royal chronicles.

*Original Scripture [Esther 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EST02.htm).*

## Chapter 3 — A Decree of Destruction

The king promoted Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite above other officials. Everyone at the gate bowed, but Mordecai refused because he was Jewish.

Haman became furious. Rather than attack Mordecai alone, he planned to destroy all Jews throughout the empire. In the twelfth year, during Nisan, people cast Pur - the lot - before Haman, selecting the thirteenth day of Adar.

Haman told the king a scattered people had different laws and disobeyed royal law. He offered ten thousand talents of silver for their destruction. The king gave Haman his signet ring and authority.

On Nisan's thirteenth day, scribes sent sealed decrees in every language ordering the destruction of all Jews, young and old, women and children, and seizure of their possessions on Adar's thirteenth.

The king and Haman drank while Susa was bewildered.

*Original Scripture [Esther 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EST03.htm).*

## Chapter 4 — For Such a Time as This

Mordecai tore his clothes, wore sackcloth and ashes, and cried through Susa. Across provinces Jews fasted, wept, and mourned.

Esther sent clothes, but Mordecai refused them. Through Hathach she learned the decree and Haman's promised silver. Mordecai sent a copy and asked her to plead with the king.

Esther replied that anyone approaching the inner court uninvited could die unless the king extended the golden scepter. She had not been summoned for thirty days.

Mordecai warned that palace walls would not save her. Relief and deliverance would arise for Jews from another place, but she and her father's house could perish. Who knew whether she had reached royalty for such a time?

Esther asked all Jews in Susa to fast without food or drink for three days and nights. She and her attendants would fast too. Then she would go unlawfully to the king: if she perished, she perished.

*Original Scripture [Esther 4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EST04.htm).*

## Chapter 5 — Two Banquets and a Gallows

On the third day Esther dressed royally and stood in the inner court. The king extended his golden scepter and offered up to half the kingdom.

She invited the king and Haman to a banquet. There the king asked her request. Esther invited them to a second banquet next day, promising her answer.

Haman left joyful, but seeing Mordecai neither rise nor tremble filled him with rage. At home he boasted to Zeresh and friends about wealth, sons, promotion, and exclusive invitations. Yet Mordecai's presence made everything feel worthless.

Zeresh and the friends advised building a gallows fifty cubits high and asking the king to hang Mordecai before the banquet. Haman liked the idea and built it.

*Original Scripture [Esther 5](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EST05.htm).*

## Chapter 6 — The Night the King Could Not Sleep

That night the king could not sleep and ordered the chronicles read. He heard how Mordecai exposed Bigthan and Teresh. No honor had been given.

Haman entered to request Mordecai's death. Before he could speak, the king asked how to honor someone the king delighted in. Assuming himself, Haman proposed royal clothing, the king's horse and crown, and a noble publicly announcing the honor.

The king ordered Haman to do everything for Mordecai the Jew at the gate. Haman clothed Mordecai, led him through the city, and proclaimed the royal honor.

Mordecai returned to the gate. Haman hurried home mourning with covered head. Zeresh and his advisers warned that if Mordecai was Jewish, Haman had begun a fall he could not overcome.

Before they finished, eunuchs rushed Haman to Esther's banquet.

*Original Scripture [Esther 6](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EST06.htm).*

## Chapter 7 — Haman Falls on His Own Gallows

At the second banquet the king again asked Esther's request. She pleaded for her life and her people's lives: they had been sold for destruction. Silence might have been possible under slavery, but annihilation threatened the king's interests too.

The king demanded the culprit's identity.

Esther named the adversary: wicked Haman.

Terrified, Haman watched the king leave angrily for the garden. He fell upon Esther's couch begging. The returning king interpreted it as assault.

Harbonah mentioned the fifty-cubit gallows Haman built for Mordecai, who had saved the king.

The king ordered Haman hanged upon it. Then his anger subsided.

*Original Scripture [Esther 7](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EST07.htm).*

## Chapter 8 — A Counter-Decree

The king gave Haman's estate to Esther. Mordecai entered the king's presence because Esther revealed their relationship. The king gave Mordecai Haman's signet ring, and Esther placed him over the estate.

Esther again fell weeping before the king, begging him to reverse Haman's letters. The golden scepter extended.

Persian decrees sealed by the king could not be revoked, so the king authorized Esther and Mordecai to write another.

On Sivan's twenty-third day, scribes sent messages through 127 provinces by swift royal horses. Jews could gather, defend themselves, destroy armed attackers, and take plunder on Adar's thirteenth.

Mordecai left wearing blue and white royal garments, a great gold crown, and purple linen. Susa rejoiced.

For Jews there was light, gladness, joy, and honor. Feasts spread through provinces, and many people declared themselves Jews because fear of them had fallen.

*Original Scripture [Esther 8](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EST08.htm).*

## Chapter 9 — The Days Called Purim

On Adar's thirteenth, when enemies hoped to rule Jews, the situation reversed. Jews assembled, and officials helped because fear of Mordecai spread; he had become powerful.

In Susa they killed five hundred men and Haman's ten sons but took no plunder. Esther requested another day in Susa and public display of the sons' bodies. On the fourteenth they killed three hundred more without plunder. Across provinces Jews killed seventy-five thousand enemies, again taking no plunder.

Provincial Jews rested and feasted on the fourteenth; Susa rested on the fifteenth.

Mordecai established annual celebration on Adar's fourteenth and fifteenth, days when sorrow turned to joy and mourning to holiday, marked by feasts, gifts to one another, and help for poor people.

They called the days Purim after Pur, Haman's lot. Esther and Mordecai confirmed the observance with authority, and letters of peace and truth established fasting, lament, and celebration for every generation.

*Original Scripture [Esther 9](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EST09.htm).*

## Chapter 10 — Mordecai Seeks His People's Good

King Ahasuerus imposed tribute across the empire and distant coastlands.

His acts of power and the full account of Mordecai's greatness were written in the chronicles of Media and Persia.

Mordecai the Jew stood second only to the king. He was great among the Jews and loved by many of his people because he sought their good and spoke peace for all their descendants.

*Original Scripture [Esther 10](https://ebible.org/engwebp/EST10.htm).*
