# James

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold - A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The public-domain Protestant World English Bible is the source. Read this adaptation alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 — Hearing That Becomes Doing

James, a servant of God and Jesus Christ, greeted the twelve tribes scattered among the nations.

They should treat trials as an occasion for joy, because tested faith produces endurance. Endurance must finish its work, making them mature. Anyone lacking wisdom should ask God, who gives generously without scolding, but ask in faith rather than being divided like a sea wave driven by wind.

The poor believer could boast in being raised, and the rich in being humbled, for wealth fades like a flower under burning sun. The person enduring trial is blessed and will receive the crown of life.

No one should blame God for temptation. God is not tempted by evil and tempts no one. Desire draws and traps a person; conceived desire gives birth to sin, and completed sin produces death.

Every good gift comes from the Father of lights, who does not shift like a shadow. By truth's word he gave them new birth as a firstfruits of creation.

They should be quick to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger. Human anger does not produce God's righteousness. They must receive the planted word humbly and become doers, not hearers who deceive themselves. A hearer-only resembles someone who studies a mirror and immediately forgets his face.

True religion controls the tongue, cares for orphans and widows in distress, and remains unstained by the world.

*Original Scripture [James 1](https://ebible.org/engwebp/JAS01.htm).*

## Chapter 2 — Faith Made Visible

Believers in glorious Jesus Christ must not practice favoritism. If a richly dressed visitor received honor while a poor person was told to stand aside or sit at someone's feet, the assembly had become judges with evil thoughts.

God chose the world's poor to be rich in faith and heirs of the promised kingdom, yet the rich often oppressed them and dishonored Christ's name. The royal law, "Love your neighbor as yourself," was good; favoritism broke the law.

Whoever kept the whole law but failed at one point became accountable. The God who forbade adultery also forbade murder. They should speak and act as people judged by the law of freedom. Judgment is merciless toward the merciless, but mercy triumphs over judgment.

What good was claimed faith without action? Words alone could not warm or feed a brother or sister lacking clothes and food. Faith without works was dead.

Mere belief that God is one was not enough; demons believed and trembled. Abraham's faith worked with his actions when he offered Isaac, and Scripture called him God's friend. Rahab the prostitute likewise acted by receiving the messengers and sending them another way.

As a body without spirit is dead, faith without works is dead.

*Original Scripture [James 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/JAS02.htm).*

## Chapter 3 — A Small Flame, a Great Forest

Not many should become teachers, for teachers face stricter judgment and everyone stumbles in speech. A person able to control words could guide the whole body.

A bit directs a horse; a small rudder turns a great ship despite fierce winds. The tongue is similarly small yet boasts greatly. One flame can ignite a forest. The tongue can become a world of wrongdoing, setting life's course on fire.

Humans tame every kind of animal, bird, reptile, and sea creature, but no one can tame the tongue. With it they bless God and curse people made in God's likeness. Blessing and cursing should not flow from the same mouth, just as one spring cannot produce fresh and bitter water, nor a fig tree olives, a vine figs, or salt water fresh.

True wisdom appears through good conduct and gentle humility. Bitter jealousy and selfish ambition must not masquerade as wisdom; they are earthly, unspiritual, and demonic, producing disorder and evil.

Wisdom from above is pure, peaceful, gentle, reasonable, full of mercy and good fruit, impartial, and sincere. Peacemakers sow peace and harvest righteousness.

*Original Scripture [James 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/JAS03.htm).*

## Chapter 4 — Draw Near to God

Quarrels came from desires fighting within them. They wanted, envied, fought, and failed to receive because they did not ask - or asked selfishly to spend on pleasures.

Friendship with the world's rebellious values was hostility toward God. Yet God gives greater grace. Scripture says he opposes the proud and gives grace to the humble.

They should submit to God, resist the devil, and he would flee. Draw near to God and he would draw near. They should clean their hands, purify divided hearts, mourn sin, and humble themselves; the Lord would lift them up.

No one should speak against a brother or judge the law. There is one Lawgiver and Judge, able to save and destroy. Who were they to judge a neighbor?

Merchants should not boast, "Today or tomorrow we will travel, trade for a year, and profit." They did not know tomorrow. Life was mist appearing briefly and vanishing. They should say, "If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that."

Proud plans were evil. Whoever knew the good to do and refused it sinned.

*Original Scripture [James 4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/JAS04.htm).*

## Chapter 5 — Patient until the Harvest

The oppressive rich should weep: stored wealth had rotted, clothes were moth-eaten, and corroded gold testified against them. Wages withheld from harvest workers cried out, and the Lord of Armies heard the reapers. They had lived luxuriously while condemning the righteous.

Believers should wait patiently for the Lord, like a farmer awaiting autumn and spring rains. They must strengthen their hearts, stop grumbling against one another, and remember the Judge stood at the door.

The prophets were examples of patient suffering. They had heard of Job's endurance and the Lord's compassionate purpose. Above all, they must not swear by heaven, earth, or anything else; their yes should be yes and no, no.

The suffering should pray, the cheerful sing praise, and the sick call church elders. The elders should pray and anoint with oil in the Lord's name. Faithful prayer would raise the sick, and forgiven sin would restore. They should confess to one another and pray for healing.

Elijah was human like them. He prayed, rain stopped for three years and six months; he prayed again, and earth produced fruit.

Whoever turned a wandering sinner back from error would save a life from death and cover many sins.

*Original Scripture [James 5](https://ebible.org/engwebp/JAS05.htm).*
