# Amos

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold - A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The public-domain Protestant World English Bible is the source. Read this adaptation alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 @@ The Lord Roars from Zion

Amos, a shepherd from Tekoa, received words about Israel during Uzziah of Judah and Jeroboam son of Joash of Israel, two years before the earthquake. The Lord roared from Zion; pastures mourned and Carmel withered.

For three crimes and four, judgment would not turn back. Damascus had threshed Gilead with iron, so fire would consume Hazael's house, break Damascus's gate, and send Aram into exile.

Gaza had deported whole communities to Edom, so fire would consume Philistine walls and rulers. Tyre likewise delivered allied communities to Edom and forgot a brotherly covenant.

Edom pursued his brother with the sword and nursed anger without end; fire would consume Teman and Bozrah.

Ammon ripped open pregnant women in Gilead to enlarge territory. Rabbah would burn amid battle cries and storm, and its king and officials would go into exile.

*Original Scripture [Amos 1](https://ebible.org/engwebp/AMO01.htm).*

## Chapter 2 @@ Israel Sold the Righteous

Moab burned Edom's king's bones into lime, so fire and battle would consume Kerioth and Moab's ruler.

Judah rejected the Lord's law and followed ancestral lies; fire would consume Jerusalem.

Israel's charge was longer: they sold righteous people for silver and the poor for sandals, trampled the vulnerable, denied justice, and father and son exploited the same woman. Beside every altar they lay on pledged garments and drank wine bought through fines.

Yet God had destroyed the tall, strong Amorite, brought Israel from Egypt through forty wilderness years, raised prophets and Nazirites. Israel made Nazirites drink wine and silenced prophets.

God would press them like a cart loaded with grain. The swift could not flee, the strong save themselves, archers stand, riders escape, or courageous keep their clothes.

*Original Scripture [Amos 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/AMO02.htm).*

## Chapter 3 @@ The Lion Has Roared

God addressed the family brought from Egypt: because he had known them uniquely, he would punish their sins.

A chain of questions showed cause and consequence: companions meet by agreement; a lion roars after finding prey; a trap springs when triggered; a trumpet alarms a city. When the Lord reveals his plan to prophets, prophecy follows as surely as fear follows a lion's roar.

Ashdod and Egypt should witness Samaria's disorder, violence, and stored plunder. An enemy would surround and strip its strength.

As a shepherd rescues only two legs or an ear from a lion, so little would remain of Samaria's luxury. God would punish Bethel's altars, cutting off their horns. Winter and summer houses, ivory houses, and great homes would end.

*Original Scripture [Amos 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/AMO03.htm).*

## Chapter 4 @@ Yet You Did Not Return

The wealthy women of Samaria were called cows of Bashan: they oppressed poor people and demanded drink. They would be taken through breaches with hooks.

Israel could keep sinning at Bethel and Gilgal, loudly presenting sacrifices, tithes, leavened thanks, and voluntary offerings.

God had sent empty stomachs, drought distributed town by town, blight, mildew, locusts, plague like Egypt's, battlefield deaths, and overthrow like Sodom and Gomorrah. Each refrain remained: yet they did not return.

Therefore Israel must prepare to meet its God, the One forming mountains, creating wind, revealing thoughts, turning dawn to darkness, and walking earth's heights.

*Original Scripture [Amos 4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/AMO04.htm).*

## Chapter 5 @@ Let Justice Roll

Amos sang a funeral song: virgin Israel had fallen with no one to raise her; only a tenth of troops would return.

The Lord said, seek me and live. Do not seek Bethel, Gilgal, or Beersheba; false sanctuaries would fall. They turned justice into bitterness and threw righteousness down.

The Creator made Pleiades and Orion, turned darkness to morning, called sea waters onto earth, and destroyed strongholds. Yet people hated correction at the gate, trampled poor people, taxed grain, built stone houses they would not inhabit, and planted vineyards whose wine they would not drink.

God knew their many sins: bribery, oppression, and denied justice. Seek good, not evil; establish justice at the gate. Perhaps God would show mercy to Joseph's remnant.

The Lord's day would be darkness, like escaping a lion only to meet a bear, or resting a hand on a wall and being bitten by a snake.

God rejected their festivals, offerings, and songs. Let justice roll like water and righteousness like a never-failing stream. Because they carried idols, exile would go beyond Damascus.

*Original Scripture [Amos 5](https://ebible.org/engwebp/AMO05.htm).*

## Chapter 6 @@ Woe to the Complacent

Woe to those comfortable in Zion and secure on Samaria's mountain, leaders trusted by Israel. They should compare Calneh, Hamath, and Gath.

They pushed away disaster while bringing violence near. They lounged on ivory beds, ate choice lambs and calves, improvised music like David, drank wine by bowls, and used fine oils, but did not grieve Joseph's ruin.

They would be first into exile; their feasting would end.

The Lord detested Jacob's pride and would surrender the city. If ten remained in one house, they would die; survivors feared even naming the Lord.

Horses do not run on rocks, nor oxen plow the sea, yet Israel turned justice into poison and righteousness into bitterness. They boasted over Lo Debar and Karnaim, but God would raise a nation to oppress them from Lebo Hamath to the Arabah.

*Original Scripture [Amos 6](https://ebible.org/engwebp/AMO06.htm).*

## Chapter 7 @@ The Plumb Line and Amaziah

God showed Amos locusts forming after the king's harvest. Amos begged forgiveness for small Jacob, and God relented.

Then God showed consuming fire drying the deep and land. Amos pleaded again, and God relented.

In a third vision, the Lord stood by a wall with a plumb line. Israel would no longer be spared; Isaac's high places and Israel's sanctuaries would fall, and Jeroboam's house face the sword.

Amaziah, Bethel's priest, accused Amos before Jeroboam of conspiracy, reporting the prophecy against king and exile. Amaziah ordered the seer to flee to Judah, earn bread there, and never prophesy at the royal sanctuary.

Amos answered that he had not been a professional prophet but a herdsman and dresser of sycamore figs. The Lord took him from the flock and sent him to Israel.

Because Amaziah silenced God's word, his family and land would suffer, he would die in an unclean country, and Israel would certainly go into exile.

*Original Scripture [Amos 7](https://ebible.org/engwebp/AMO07.htm).*

## Chapter 8 @@ A Basket of Summer Fruit

God showed Amos summer fruit, a wordplay announcing Israel's end. Temple songs would become wailing, with bodies everywhere and silence.

Merchants trampled needy people, impatient for sacred days to end so they could sell grain with smaller measures, higher prices, dishonest scales, buying poor people for sandals and selling sweepings as wheat.

God swore not to forget. The land would tremble and rise like the Nile.

That day, sun would set at noon. Festivals would become mourning, songs lament, clothing sackcloth, heads shaved, grief like losing an only child.

A famine would come, not of bread or water but hearing the Lord's words. People would wander sea to sea seeking a message and not find it. Those swearing by Samaria's shame, Dan's god, or Beersheba's way would fall permanently.

*Original Scripture [Amos 8](https://ebible.org/engwebp/AMO08.htm).*

## Chapter 9 @@ The Fallen Shelter Raised

Amos saw the Lord beside the altar commanding its capitals struck. No one could hide in grave, heaven, Carmel, sea, or captivity; God's eyes would find them. The Lord touched earth and it melted, rising like the Nile.

Was Israel's exodus the only migration God governed? He also moved Philistines from Caphtor and Arameans from Kir. The sinful kingdom would be destroyed, though Jacob's house would not vanish completely.

Israel would be shaken among nations like grain in a sieve without a pebble falling. Complacent sinners would die.

Yet God would raise David's fallen shelter, repair ruins, and rebuild it. The restored people would possess Edom's remnant and nations bearing God's name.

Days would come when harvests overlapped: plowman with reaper, grape treader with sower; mountains would drip sweet wine. God would restore Israel, and they would rebuild cities, plant vineyards and gardens, and enjoy their fruit. He would plant them in their land, never again uprooted.

*Original Scripture [Amos 9](https://ebible.org/engwebp/AMO09.htm).*
