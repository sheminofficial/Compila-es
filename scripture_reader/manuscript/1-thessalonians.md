# 1 Thessalonians

Editorial manuscript for *The Bible, Retold - A Literary Adaptation for Young English Readers*. The public-domain Protestant World English Bible is the source. Read this adaptation alongside Scripture.

## Chapter 1 @@ Faith, Love, and Hope

Paul, Silvanus, and Timothy wrote to the Thessalonian church in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, offering grace and peace.

They continually thanked God for everyone, remembering their work of faith, labor of love, and endurance of hope. God had chosen them: the good news arrived not as words alone but with power, the Holy Spirit, and deep conviction.

Despite severe suffering, they received the message with Spirit-given joy and imitated the missionaries and the Lord. They became an example throughout Macedonia and Achaia. Their faith sounded outward so clearly that Paul needed to add nothing.

People reported how the Thessalonians welcomed the messengers, turned from idols to serve the living and true God, and waited for his Son from heaven - Jesus, raised from the dead, who rescues from coming judgment.

*Original Scripture [1 Thessalonians 1](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1TH01.htm).*

## Chapter 2 @@ Gentle as a Mother, Encouraging as a Father

Their visit had not failed. After suffering in Philippi, Paul and his companions spoke courageously in Thessalonica amid conflict. Their appeal came without error, impurity, deception, flattery, greed, or a search for human praise. God had entrusted them with the gospel and examined their hearts.

Though apostles could assert authority, they were gentle like a nursing mother caring for her children. Their affection was so deep that they shared not only God's message but their own lives. They worked night and day to avoid burdening anyone.

Their conduct was holy, righteous, and blameless. Like a father with his children, they encouraged and urged each believer to walk worthily of God, who calls people into his kingdom and glory.

Paul thanked God that they received the preached word as God's word, which worked within believers. They suffered from their own countrymen as Judean churches had suffered.

Paul had been separated from them in person, not heart. He repeatedly tried to return, but Satan hindered him. At Jesus' coming, the Thessalonians themselves would be his hope, joy, and crown.

*Original Scripture [1 Thessalonians 2](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1TH02.htm).*

## Chapter 3 @@ Timothy Brings Good News

Unable to endure uncertainty, Paul chose to remain alone in Athens and sent Timothy, God's coworker in Christ's gospel, to strengthen and encourage their faith. They should not be shaken by suffering; believers had been warned that affliction would come.

Paul feared the tempter might have undone their labor. But Timothy returned with good news of their faith and love. They remembered the missionaries kindly and longed to see them.

Their steadfast faith comforted Paul in his own distress. He felt alive because they stood firm in the Lord. His joy overflowed into thanksgiving. Night and day he prayed intensely to see them and supply what their faith still lacked.

May God the Father and Jesus direct the journey to them. May the Lord make their love increase and overflow toward one another and everyone, establishing blameless, holy hearts when Jesus comes with all his holy ones.

*Original Scripture [1 Thessalonians 3](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1TH03.htm).*

## Chapter 4 @@ A Quiet Life and a Living Hope

They had learned how to live pleasingly before God and should progress still more. God's will was their holiness: avoiding sexual immorality, controlling their bodies honorably rather than in uncontrolled passion, and never exploiting another. God called them not to impurity but holiness; rejecting this instruction rejected God, who gives his Holy Spirit.

They already practiced brotherly love across Macedonia, but should increase. They should aim for a quiet life, mind their own affairs, and work with their hands, gaining outsiders' respect and avoiding needless dependence.

They must not grieve hopelessly for believers who had died. Since Jesus died and rose, God would bring with him those asleep in Jesus.

At the Lord's command, an archangel's voice, and God's trumpet, the Lord would descend. The dead in Christ would rise first; then living believers would be caught up with them in the clouds to meet the Lord. They would always be with him. They should comfort one another with these words.

*Original Scripture [1 Thessalonians 4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1TH04.htm).*

## Chapter 5 @@ Children of Light

They did not need dates and times: the Lord's day would come like a thief at night. While people announced peace and safety, destruction would arrive suddenly like labor pains.

But believers were not in darkness. They were children of light and day, so they should remain awake and sober. Wearing faith and love as a breastplate and salvation's hope as a helmet, they remembered God appointed them not for wrath but salvation through Jesus, who died so they might live with him.

They should encourage and build one another up; respect leaders who labored and admonished them; live peacefully; warn the disorderly, encourage the fainthearted, support the weak, and remain patient. They must never repay evil with evil but pursue good.

Rejoice always. Pray without ceasing. Give thanks in every circumstance. Do not quench the Spirit or despise prophecies; test everything, hold firmly to good, and avoid every form of evil.

May the God of peace sanctify them completely and preserve spirit, soul, and body blameless for Jesus' coming. The one calling them was faithful and would do it.

Paul requested prayer, holy greetings, and the public reading of the letter. May Jesus' grace be with them.

*Original Scripture [1 Thessalonians 5](https://ebible.org/engwebp/1TH05.htm).*
